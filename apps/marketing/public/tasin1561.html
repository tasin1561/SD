SEC 01The wedge

What Skydrop is

Selling into India from Bangladesh fails on six fronts at once — no Indian warehouse, no courier contract, no COD call-confirmation, no warehouse operation, no money rail, no local support. Building all of it alone takes 6+ months, ₹50 lakh+, and an Indian company. Skydrop is the shared operating system that removes every one of those blockers, so the seller only sells.

Seller · Bangladesh

A company account with many staff/agent logins under one roof — role-scoped (owner, ops, inventory, finance, viewer). Onboard, manage catalog & stock, place or bulk-upload orders, watch shipments, resolve exceptions, manage an INR wallet, and withdraw earnings.

Skydrop staff · India

The internal console — approve sellers, confirm orders by phone, receive & label stock, pick/pack under scan discipline, book couriers across multiple accounts, run returns, scrap & support tickets, and settle payouts.

Customer · India

An ordinary Indian delivery, phone-confirmed, with public parcel tracking in English and Hindi. They never see there's a border in the middle.

SEC 02The differentiator

Inventory integrity, down to the unit

Cross-border trust lives or dies on inventory accuracy — the seller's stock is in someone else's country. Skydrop runs a real WMS where every movement is a scan, and offers two integrity modes per seller.

Normal mode foundation live

One barcode per SKU. Ten identical bottles share one code, tracked as a quantity across bins and batches — fast, cheap, ideal for fungible goods.

  • Bins, batches, FIFO/FEFO, append-only stock ledger
  • Low-stock alerts, cycle counts, receiving flows

Strict mode designed

One unique barcode per physical unit. Ten bottles carry ten codes, applied by Skydrop at receiving and followed individually — through pick, pack, dispatch, and all the way to RTO.

  • Serialized unit ledger; each item auditable end-to-end
  • Damaged-label recovery on returns; missing-unit detection

Virtual vs physical designed

Physical inventory is confirmed by scan at pack. Virtual inventory can be booked earlier — at order placement or at call-confirmation — as a per-seller option, with auto- or seller-decided release when a customer can't be reached.

Scrap & damage tickets designed

Any unit damaged in operations or on return moves to a scrap hold and raises a ticket — settled with the seller and courier by reimbursement or physical return, never silently written off.

Multi-warehouse network designed

Stock lives across warehouses by product. Returns can be received at any location, entered, and routed to the item's designated warehouse — the network stays consistent.

Why it compounds: every gate — receive, pick, pack, hand-off, return — is a barcode scan tied to the order's invoice. That single discipline is what makes strict serialization, scrap accountability, and multi-warehouse routing trustworthy rather than aspirational.
SEC 03The money engine

Finance & settlement, accurate to the rupee

Money is the part that can never be wrong. Skydrop runs on INR as the canonical currency and a double-entry ledger where every rupee reconciles to zero — so a Bangladeshi seller is paid quickly and correctly for Indian COD sales, and we always know exactly how much cash we've fronted and are owed.

The COD settlement waterfall designed

Wallet & withdrawals foundation live

The per-currency wallet ledger + live balance runs today. Adding self-service withdrawals (min threshold + daily count limit), auto-withdraw cycles, and instant credit-at-delivery as a paid priority add-on.

Advance credit & float designed

Couriers pay us 5–10 days after delivery; we credit the seller ~2 days after (per-seller). Every advanced rupee is tracked as float, with auto-clawback if a courier remittance doesn't reconcile.

Margin, by design designed

We fetch the real per-parcel courier fee and book our margin; the seller only ever sees the blended charge. Per-seller fee (flat or %), admin-set, charged at courier-entry or at payout.

Currency FX live

INR canonical; BDT via a manual, audited INR↔BDT rate with full history. Sellers enter product cost in BDT or INR — stored as entered, converted live for display.

Three price components designed

① Product cost (BDT/INR, record-only valuation) · ② inbound freight (BD → IN warehouse, may be 0) · ③ dues — pay-on-arrival or pay-later, deducted at payout (future 0.5–1% service charge).

Reconciliation designed

Courier remittance files matched parcel-by-parcel against what we advanced; a daily close proves the books; per-seller exposure and global float on one dashboard.

Why double-entry: every rupee credited to a seller posts as a balanced debit to a Skydrop float / receivable account. The system can prove, at any instant, exactly how much cash it has fronted and is owed — nothing is booked twice, nothing goes missing. This is the discipline behind “never lose a rupee.”
SEC 04The operational pipeline

How an order flows, end-to-end

Every order travels one instrumented lifecycle, enforced by a state machine — no “any-to-any” jumps — with an append-only event log and stock conserved so it decrements exactly once, at dispatch. Amber stages are the refined operating model now in build.

01 · stock in
Receive & label
Goods arrive; in strict mode Skydrop applies a per-unit barcode. Scanned into bins.
02 · order
Order placed
Single or CSV. Recipient + SKU snapshotted immutably; optional virtual booking holds stock now.
03 · confirm
Call-confirm
Round-robin queue rings the buyer. Unreachable → seller decides: keep trying or unbook.
04 · courier
Courier-first booking
AWB booked via the seller's dedicated courier account (one of several, split by ratio) the moment it's confirmed. Rejection → fallback panel → another account or carrier.
05 · pick
Scan-gated pick
Only booked orders reach the floor. Picker scans each unit against the invoice.
06 · pack
Scan-gated pack
Scan items, then scan the invoice to seal. Orders kept separate by courier.
07 · hand-off
Scan-gated handover
Every parcel re-scanned before the courier takes custody. Nothing leaves unscanned.
08 · track
Track & deliver
Courier scans drive the timeline; public AWB lookup for the buyer, EN + हिन्दी.
09 · returns
RTO intake
Received at any warehouse, entered, and routed to the designated one; damaged labels recovered.
10 · resolve
Scrap & settle
Healthy units restock; damaged units raise a scrap ticket, settled by money or return.
11 · money
Settle & pay
Collected COD runs the settlement waterfall; the net is credited to the seller's wallet in advance, withdrawable on request or auto-cycle.

The engineering discipline that holds it together: a saga pattern (“visible-vs-silent failure ordering”) across every cross-module step, so a crash mid-flow always leaves a self-announcing, recoverable state; single-source mapping services for call-outcome, tracking-scan, and notification fan-out; and a permanent stock-conservation regression test proving inventory is never inflated or leaked.

SEC 06The build ahead

Roadmap to the target operating model

The refinement is additive — the schema was designed forward-compatible, and the reservation, warehouse, courier, and settings seams already exist. Four focused phases close the gap between the shipped foundation and the operating model above.

Phase A · Finance core & currency designed

Upgrade the wallet to a true double-entry ledger; advance-credit with per-seller exposure tracking + auto-clawback; self-service & auto withdrawals plus an instant-credit add-on; real courier-fee capture with margin; per-seller courier fee (flat/%); the three price components; BDT/INR product-cost entry; and courier-remittance reconciliation.

Phase B · Inventory integrity designed

Serialized per-unit model behind a per-seller normal/strict toggle; barcode labels at receiving; mandatory scan gates at pick, pack and hand-off; and a general per-seller settings framework — including call config (attempts, min interval, no-call mode) under an admin-enforced cap.

Phase C · Courier network & dispatch designed

Multi-account couriers with seller↔account linkage, per-seller split ratios and full parcel→account tracing; courier-first booking after call-confirm; and a fallback panel to re-book a rejected order into another account or carrier. Pack floor partitioned by courier.

Phase D · Network, returns & exceptions designed

Multi-warehouse routing + cross-warehouse RTO transfer; virtual booking with seller-decided unbook; scrap + damage tickets and a seller support-ticket panel; a warehouse scanner app; and an exception dashboard unifying missing/mismatch/damaged/failed-courier/unbook queues.

Why this is low-risk: none of it is a rewrite. The wallet ledger, COD accrual, FX and 18% GST engine already run in production; reservations already model virtual holds; the warehouse already has bins, batches and quarantine/damaged bin types; the courier layer already has a fallback; and per-seller overrides already exist. Each phase generalizes a seam that is already load-bearing in production.

SEC 08Load & scale

Capacity today, and the path to scale

The current deployment is sized for pre-launch beta and sits comfortably inside it — the droplet is near-idle at rest. The honest ceiling is set by two deliberate single-instance choices, not by the design.

throughput headroom · single-instance topology
beta · comfortable steady early traffic first bottleneck needs scale-out

Comfortable today

  • Tens of active sellers and hundreds to low-thousands of orders/day
  • Concurrent call-center agents and warehouse staff on the queues
  • Light reads served in low single-digit milliseconds; idle at rest
  • Public tracking is per-IP rate-limited and cheap — the highest-volume anonymous surface

The scaling path designed-for

  • Move the 13 background workers into the dedicated worker process
  • Run the API in cluster mode / behind a load balancer (rate-limiting already supports a shared Redis store)
  • Upgrade managed Postgres + add read replicas; promote Redis to managed
  • None require re-architecture — they are the intended next steps, deferred because beta load doesn't need them
