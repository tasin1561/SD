# Inbound freight (FRT-1..7)

10. **FRT-1 (R3): inbound freight is a SEPARATE money flow from the outbound courier fee, and is a PER-UNIT LANDED COST.** A consignment's bill is split across its lines BY WEIGHT (freight is priced by weight; a line whose SKU has no recorded weight falls back to a count share, and a zero weight is treated as MISSING — a missing weight must never make freight free), snapshotted at record time in `inbound_freight_allocations` so a later catalog edit cannot retro-price landed goods. A unit's share is charged when that unit LEAVES — delivered (in the DELIVERED accrual tx) or written off at RTO (best-effort; the freight was really spent, and compensation for the lost goods stays manual) — so the rest of the consignment still owes. Attribution is `shipment_item.pickedBatchId → stock_batch → goods_receipt_lines.batchId → allocation`, walking `parentBatchId` so an R6b child batch resolves to its parent's line. ONE `INBOUND_FREIGHT` wallet entry per order is the idempotency gate (an order can span consignments). PAY_NOW is charged in full at record time and NEVER amortised; `settle` charges only the outstanding remainder.
11. **FRT-2 (R3): the separation itself.** `INBOUND_FREIGHT` (per-consignment, cross-border) is never merged with `ORDER_CHARGES` (per-order, India-domestic) — the two cost bases must stay separable in reporting. It is always a DEBIT, so it is deliberately ABSENT from `CREDIT_DIRECTIONS` on BOTH sides (see WAL-1). One bill per goods receipt (UNIQUE `goods_receipt_id` IS the record idempotency gate); `settle` re-guards on `status=PENDING` inside the tx so two operators can't double-debit; the UNIQUE `wallet_entry_id` is the charged-exactly-once evidence. The pay-later service-charge percent is SNAPSHOTTED at record time, never re-resolved at settlement. WAIVED is distinct from SETTLED so forgiven money stays countable.

13. **FRT-3 (2026-09-12): a freight line owes its GROSS share** (`line_gross_inr`, including its share of the pay-later service charge; the lines sum to `total_inr` exactly). Charged against the pre-service-charge figure, a bill cleared by deliveries closed SETTLED short and `settle` then refused, so the service charge was reported as revenue and never taken. Amortisation STOPS at a SETTLED, WAIVED or PAY_NOW bill — it kept charging after `settle`, which billed the rest of the consignment twice (₹10,000 bill, 20 units then settle then 80 more: ₹18,000). `settle` reads the remainder under the WALLET lock inside its claim and marks every line fully charged in the same transaction. **AMENDED 2026-09-12 — a bill closes on its MONEY, never its units.** `rollUpCharge` marks SETTLED only when `amount_settled_inr >= total_inr`; a bill charged at pre-gross rates could otherwise close with every unit gone and part of its total uncollectable. The charge that takes a bill's LAST unit collects every line's leftover on that unit and marks every line fully charged, so a bill's lifetime total is exactly `total_inr`; a bill whose units are all gone but whose money is not stays PARTIALLY_SETTLED, where `settle` charges the remainder. Pinned by `inbound-freight-amortisation-close.spec.ts` (a stateful in-memory bill — a mocked Prisma cannot show it).

14. **FRT-4 (2026-09-12): our cost for a freight bill is the SUM of its forwarder payments**, each converted at the rate in force at its OWN instant (`fx_rate_history`, else `fx_rates` — the P&L's rule), recomputed on every pay or attribute, the rate and source on the entry's note. It is never typed (`costInr` is gone from both DTOs): only the first payment used to count, and the one typed figure on production implied 1.20 BDT/INR where every recorded rate said 1.23. `setOurCost` is for an invoice with no payment yet. Every re-sum of a bill's cost (pay, attribute, and `setOurCost`'s no-payments check) runs under `AdvisoryLock.FREIGHT_COST` on the bill — without it two payments at once each re-summed only their own uncommitted entry and one dropped off our cost and the P&L. The per-payment rupee figures are stamped with the total (`our_cost_payments`) and the breakdown reads them, so nothing re-prices the cost on read.

15. **FRT-5 (2026-09-20): PAY_ADVANCE — billed at the Bangladesh intake, before the goods fly.** The owner: *"We tell the seller the freight cost before he sends it to our BD warehouse — per kg or per pcs. Then in BD we do the count or weight. Then we give the final bill to the seller and the wallet is debited. After it reaches India, after all the expenses and forwarder cost, it can be loss or profit."* The rate is agreed BY PHONE and typed at billing time; there is no quote object and no reconciliation with the seller — the gap between what we bill and what the forwarder costs is **Skydrop's margin**, not a correction. **It is not a new billing MECHANISM**: the invoice is priced line by line, per kg or per piece, at a rate ops types after the count, exactly as the other two modes are. What differs is WHICH goods receipt the bill hangs on — the `BD_INTAKE` leg rather than the India arrival — and therefore WHEN. Like PAY_NOW it debits the whole bill at record time and is never amortised (`settlesImmediately` is the ONE place that says which modes have already paid, read by the recorder AND by the amortisation walk so the two cannot disagree); unlike PAY_LATER it carries no service charge, because no credit is extended.

    **The mode is a THREE-LEVEL chain with ONE reader**: `consignment pin ?? seller override ?? global default`. The last two are SET-1's `wallet.inbound_freight_mode`, untouched and NOT rebuilt. The third is `consignments.inbound_freight_mode`, set at **BD receiving** — the moment the three modes diverge, since PAY_ADVANCE bills at the Dhaka receipt while PAY_NOW and PAY_LATER both defer to the arrival, so one decision at one point selects all three. **NULL means FALL THROUGH and is the ordinary state**; it is not a default, and the migration backfills it only for consignments that already carry a bill (from that bill's own mode — the snapshot, applied retroactively from the evidence). `ConsignmentFreightModeService` in `consignment-core` is the ONE reader and the ONE writer — the CNS-2 / BIN-1 discipline, and here the disagreement five call sites would produce is a seller billed twice or not at all. Three callers need the answer: the billing service (which leg may be billed), the dispatch guard, and the screens. `legFor` is F2-exhaustive so a fourth mode fails to compile until somebody decides which leg it bills. Resolution **FAILS to PAY_NOW** — PAY_ADVANCE would block a dispatch over a settings read and PAY_LATER would quietly extend credit. **The pin FREEZES when the bill is raised**: the billing transaction writes the effective mode in (so the consignment says what it was billed on whatever the settings do later — ORD-6 / RS-5) and `setOverride` refuses thereafter (`FREIGHT_MODE_LOCKED`, a guarded `updateMany` on "still unbilled" so a bill landing between the read and the write cannot be re-characterised). A `mode` on the record request PINS the consignment rather than forming a fourth level — a per-bill mode that did not also pin it would disagree with the consignment the moment anybody looked. **All three values are accepted at the consignment level**, not only the two that differ at Dhaka: a level of override that cannot express one of the values it overrides is a gap somebody meets later with no way round it. **SET-1 clamps INT and DECIMAL at write time and said nothing about STRINGS**, so `PAY_ADVANCED` or `pay now` saved cleanly and failed closed far away; `ENUM_VALUED_STRING_KEYS` on the resolver refuses by name (`INVALID_SETTING_VALUE`) and stores trimmed and upper-cased — the `UNKNOWN_COURIER_CODE` argument with a code enum in place of a table.

    **THE DOUBLE-BILLING GUARD is the most important part of this change.** The per-receipt key CANNOT see it: a PAY_ADVANCE bill hangs on the Dhaka intake and a PAY_NOW / PAY_LATER one on the India arrival, which are DIFFERENT rows — so both would be accepted and the seller charged twice for one consignment. `record` reads the consignment's LIVE bills and writes under `AdvisoryLock.INBOUND_FREIGHT_BILL` inside one transaction (read-then-write is not a guard under READ COMMITTED), refusing `FREIGHT_CONSIGNMENT_BILLED_IN_ADVANCE` for any second bill on an advance-billed consignment and `FREIGHT_CONSIGNMENT_ALREADY_BILLED` for an advance bill on one already billed. The partial unique `inbound_freight_one_advance_per_consignment (consignment_id) WHERE mode = 'pay_advance' AND voided_at IS NULL` is the backstop for the half an index can express — **its predicate names the new enum value, which is why the enum additions are in their OWN migration file**: Postgres refuses to use a new enum value in the transaction that added it. **"One bill" differs by mode, and the code says so**: PAY_NOW / PAY_LATER bill per ARRIVAL (a consignment lands in as many shipments as it takes — production's CN-2026-08-000003 carries two bills, CNS-6), PAY_ADVANCE bills ONCE because there is one intake. **A VOIDED bill blocks nothing, at EVERY level — that is what re-billing means**, and it took three changes to be true rather than one: the consignment guard filters `voidedAt: null`, BOTH per-receipt lookups in `record` are `findFirst` on the same predicate (the pre-flight AND the one under the lock — a predicate on one of them still lets a concurrent void through), and the two absolute uniques became partial (FRT-7).

    **The DISPATCH GUARD**: `ConsignmentDispatchService` refuses to send a PAY_ADVANCE consignment to India with no live bill (`FREIGHT_ADVANCE_NOT_BILLED`). Without it "advance" means nothing — the goods go and the only billing point left is the arrival, which a PAY_ADVANCE consignment is never billed against, so the shipment ships free. Placed BEFORE the `withoutCounting` branch on purpose: a forward-without-count has no Dhaka count for a bill to be priced from, so it is refused by exactly this rule rather than a second one saying the same thing. Same shape as CNS-6's refusal to cancel after dispatch — a rule that makes an unanswerable state unreachable.

    **The seller SEES the bill**: `record` writes `ConsignmentEventType.FREIGHT_RECORDED` to the consignment timeline — an event that had been in the vocabulary (and in apps/seller's word list, as "Freight billed") since the two-leg work with NOTHING ever writing it, so the first sign of a bill was an unexplained wallet debit. The void writes one too.

16. **FRT-6 (2026-09-20): a freight bill is AGREED in a currency and CHARGED in rupees; a wrong bill is VOIDED, never edited.** **Currency is per BILL, not per line**, because one rate was negotiated for one shipment. `RecordFreightInput.currency` (INR | BDT, default INR) and conversion through the SAME `inrRateAt` / `toInr` helpers the P&L and the flat fees use (PRC-8, TRE-6), at the BILLING INSTANT — so the rupees a seller is charged and the figure the report derives cannot diverge. The bill records `agreedAmount`, `agreedCurrency`, `fxRate`, `fxRatePair`, `fxRateSource` and `fxRateRecordedAt`; "why was I billed ₹3,658.54?" has no answer a month later otherwise. **`InboundFreightAllocation.rateInr` is RENAMED to `rate`** and `lineTotalAgreed` added beside `lineTotalInr` — the rename is the load-bearing half: the column was rupees for the whole life of the table, so a reader that kept treating it as rupees would show ৳300 as ₹300 and typecheck clean. It caught every stale site. **Lines are converted through `grossLineTotals`, not one by one**, so they sum to `amountInr` exactly; for an INR bill it is the identity and nothing about an existing bill's arithmetic moves. **A missing rate REFUSES the bill** (`NO_FX_RATE_FOR_FREIGHT`) rather than billing zero or billing the taka figure as rupees — which is a 23% overcharge that commits and renders.

    **VOID, not edit** (owner's call): editing in place would leave the wallet holding a figure the bill no longer claims and the P&L reading a total nobody was charged. `InboundFreightStatus.VOIDED` is distinct from WAIVED — a waiver is correct-and-forgiven and stays countable as money we chose not to collect; a void says the bill should never have existed. The give-back is a compensating `INBOUND_FREIGHT_REFUND` **credit** (the ledger is append-only, so a void is never a deletion) for exactly `amountSettledInr` — what was actually taken — and no entry at all when nothing was charged. WAL-1: registered in `CREDIT_DIRECTIONS` **and** routed in `isWalletCredit` / `walletDirectionLabel`. TRE-8: `TO_SELLER`, so only `max(0, after) − max(0, before)` becomes the seller's cash again. The allocations are zeroed in the same transaction (the status alone stops amortisation, but the lines are what the breakdown shows, and leaving them charged is how a withdrawn bill reads as part-paid forever); the ROWS are kept, being the record of what was billed. Reason ≥10 chars, audited HIGH, guarded `updateMany` on `(not voided, same amountSettled)`, UNIQUE `void_reversal_entry_id` as the second gate. **TRE-6: `PnlService.freightBilled` returns ZERO for a VOIDED bill** — handled at the bill rather than by subtracting refund entries, because that line is built from `inbound_freight_charges` and reads no wallet entries at all, so a refund would be invisible to it; the drill-down row survives with its stable id and a zero, which is what PNL-CF-1's close requires and what lets a month closed before the void carry the change forward. **Our COST stays on a voided bill**: we really did pay the forwarder, and withdrawing the seller's bill does not unpay them.

17. **FRT-7 (2026-09-20): "void and re-bill" has to be POSSIBLE, and an absolute unique made it impossible.** FRT-6 shipped the void and the message that tells an operator to use it — and the database refused the second half for one commit. `inbound_freight_charges.goods_receipt_id` and `inbound_freight_allocations.goods_receipt_line_id` were both UNCONDITIONALLY `@unique`, and the void deliberately KEEPS its rows as the record of what was billed, so a withdrawn bill held its receipt and its lines forever. A mistyped PAY_NOW bill left that shipment's freight **permanently uncollectable** (we absorb it, and the P&L books the forwarder cost against no revenue); a mistyped PAY_ADVANCE bill left the goods **unable to leave Bangladesh** (`FREIGHT_ADVANCE_NOT_BILLED`, with no way to raise the correct bill). Both uniques are now PARTIAL — `inbound_freight_one_live_bill_per_receipt` and `inbound_freight_one_live_allocation_per_line`, both `WHERE voided_at IS NULL` — and `inbound_freight_allocations` carries its own `voided_at`, stamped by the void in the same transaction and at the same instant as the bill's, because an index predicate cannot reach the charge's through a join.

    **Two consequences follow from the relation flipping to a LIST**, and the second is the expensive one. Prisma made `GoodsReceipt.freightCharge` and `GoodsReceiptLine.freightAllocation` to-ONE purely because of those uniques; both are now `freightCharges` / `freightAllocations`. **The attribution walk must filter in the `where`, not pick in JavaScript**: after a re-bill a receipt line carries a dead allocation beside the live one, and reading the dead one hits the walk's own `status === VOIDED` guard and returns `null` — so the unit ships **freight-free forever** with a perfectly good bill sitting next to it, which is exactly the failure `FREIGHT_LINE_MISSING` warns about in other words. `freightAllocations: { where: { voidedAt: null }, take: 1 }` narrows it at the database so the correctness cannot be "simplified" away later; the VOIDED status check stays as a backstop for a void that failed to stamp the line.

    **The unit suite CANNOT see any of this** — a mocked Prisma has no index to violate (the `pack_boxes` / `courier_pickup_requests` lesson), and the test that shipped with FRT-6 asserted re-billing worked while the database was refusing it, because its fake returned the same row for every lookup. The fakes now APPLY the `where`, and `inbound-freight-rebill.e2e-spec.ts` is the real proof: void, re-bill the same receipt, both rows coexist with one live, the wallet nets to the corrected bill, and **a unit leaving afterwards is charged at the LIVE rate** — that last one is the half that costs money, since re-billing being possible says nothing about whether the re-billed freight is ever collected.

    **A CONSIGNMENT CARRYING A LIVE BILL CANNOT BE CANCELLED** (`CONSIGNMENT_FREIGHT_BILLED`). PAY_ADVANCE makes a state CNS-6 predates reachable — a fully PAID bill on a consignment that has not flown — and `cancel` touched no freight row, so a seller could call the shipment off, get their goods back, and simply keep the debit, with no screen, no issue and nobody told. Refusing by name is the CNS-6 move (make the unanswerable state unreachable) rather than voiding inside the cancel: withdrawing a bill is a deliberate act with a reason, an audit row and a refund, and doing it as a side-effect of a different button is how a refund lands that nobody can explain. The guard reads `row.freightCharges`, which `CONSIGNMENT_INCLUDE` filters to LIVE bills, so a consignment whose bill was already withdrawn cancels freely.

    **A consignment's payload carries LIVE bills only.** The seller's header summed every row it was handed, so a refunded bill read as an outstanding charge beside a panel saying "Nothing billed yet". The filter is in the SELECT rather than at each reader — a new caller cannot forget it, which is exactly how that one arrived — and it matches `listForSeller` / `listForAdmin`, where a withdrawn bill is shown only when somebody asks for it by status. The withdrawal itself is on the consignment TIMELINE, which is where history belongs; `voidedAt`/`voidReason` are deliberately NOT in that payload, because a field that can only ever be null invites dead checks against it.

    **The dispatch guard reads INSIDE the dispatching transaction, under `AdvisoryLock.INBOUND_FREIGHT_BILL`**, and the void takes that lock BEFORE the wallet one (the order `record` already uses, so no cycle). Read outside, a void committing between the check and the write ships an unbilled advance consignment.
