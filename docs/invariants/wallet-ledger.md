**Wallet & money rules (2026-08-02 — NON-NEGOTIABLE):**

1. **WAL-2: money enters the wallet only after a human has seen it in the bank.** `WalletTopupService` writes NOTHING on submission — a seller declaring a transfer is a CLAIM, not a payment. Crediting first and reversing on rejection would let anyone raise their balance with a form, and the reversal would land after they had withdrawn against it. Accept claims the row with a guarded `updateMany` on PENDING and credits in the same tx; the UNIQUE `wallet_entry_id` is the second gate. A reference (transaction ID **or** proof upload) is mandatory — without one there is nothing to match against the statement. Proof is a Spaces KEY read through a presigned URL, namespaced per seller and checked on submit.

2. **WAL-3: the withdrawable balance is `balance − wallet.minimum_balance_inr`, and that subtraction lives in ONE method** (`WithdrawalRequestService.withdrawableBalance`). Three callers need the same number — the request guard, what the seller is shown, and the auto-sweep which withdraws exactly it. Independent subtractions drift, and the symptom is a sweep asking for money the guard refuses. Auto-withdrawal routes through the IDENTICAL guard chain as a manual request (`createAuto`): an automatic path that could take money a manual one could not is backwards. The hour is in the SELLER's timezone (`sellers.timezone`, IANA via `Intl` — never a stored offset, which drifts an hour twice a year). **WAL-3b (2026-09-12, owner): a PENDING withdrawal the wallet can no longer pay is rejected automatically; an APPROVED one is only flagged.** A pending request holds its amount out of `withdrawableBalance`; once charges land after it and the balance falls below it, nobody can pay it (approval and the remittance both refuse), yet it blocks every new request and the auto-sweep and reads as a liability — Menev Store's ₹2,946.40 auto-request of 2 Sep against ₹111.40 was exactly that. `UnpayableWithdrawalService` (every 15 min on the auto-withdrawal queue, and first inside the hourly auto-withdrawal job) rejects a PENDING request when `withdrawableBalance(…, excludeRequestId = its own id)` is below its amount — the ONE method, its own amount excluded so it cannot block itself, other pending requests still counted — judged newest-first under the WALLET lock with a guarded `updateMany` on `status = PENDING` (a concurrent approval wins). REJECTED with `resolved_by_staff_id` null is the system's shape; audited MEDIUM as SYSTEM, the seller told in-app (`wallet.withdrawal_auto_rejected`, silenceable), and it does not count toward the daily/monthly request limits. APPROVED is never auto-rejected (a transfer may be under way) — an uncovered one raises a HIGH MONEY issue that clears itself. `wallet.auto_reject_unpayable_withdrawals` (seeded TRUE, seller-overridable) switches it; an unreadable setting leaves the request pending. **AMENDED (review, 2026-09-12):** PENDING and APPROVED requests are BOTH held out of `withdrawableBalance` (an approved request held nothing, so a second request could be raised against money already promised); approval and the approved-uncovered flag exclude the request itself — approval had counted a request against its own amount, so a ₹1,000 request on a ₹1,000 wallet could never be approved. The sweep runs TWO passes: first it rejects what the balance cannot cover ON ITS OWN, then judges the rest newest-first (newest-first alone rejected a payable ₹100 request behind an unpayable ₹1,000 one). A remittance closes a matching PENDING request when none is approved. **Order charges:** the ORDER_CHARGES debit sets exactly the ESTIMATED lines it billed to CONFIRMED in its own transaction (status only; nothing reads it for money) — CONFIRMED means billed, as the RTO fee has always been written.

3. **WAL-4: GST on COD is EXTRACTED from a tax-inclusive price, never added on top.** `cod × r / (100 + r)` — ₹1,000 at 18% withholds ₹152.54, not ₹180. The wrong divisor over-withholds ~2.75% of GMV and produces a figure that cannot reconcile against a filed return. **WE file it, so the withheld amount is a LIABILITY**, recorded per order in `gst_withholdings` with the rate SNAPSHOTTED (a later change must not restate a past quarter). Netting it silently into the credit would make it read as revenue and be spent before the return is due. The arithmetic lives ONLY in `CodCreditService`. **AMENDED 2026-08-31: the wallet line carries its own `GST_WITHHOLDING` direction.** It was written as `ORDER_CHARGES` with an explanatory note, which reaches the same wrong place by another route — a note cannot be grouped by, so summing what sellers paid us in charges silently included tax we merely hold, and the seller's own ledger labelled a tax line "Order charges". It is a DEBIT (absent from `CREDIT_DIRECTIONS`, `false` from `isWalletCredit`) and `TO_CAPITAL` in `SellerCashAttributionService` — the cash stops being the seller's, which is what keeps seller-held cash equal to the wallet liability (TRE-4); that the capital side is really owed to the government is a liability the bank book does not model. **AMENDED 2026-09-07, confirmed by the founder 2026-09-11: the withheld 18% is OUR REVENUE, not a liability** — we file no return against it (the courier bills and remits GST on carriage), so the P&L reports it on its own "COD tax deduction" line with no cost side. The extraction arithmetic above still stands; only who the money belongs to changed. **The mirror image holds on the cost side:** Delhivery's and Shiprocket's debits include 18% GST that we do not reclaim, so the whole debit is the parcel's cost — never strip the GST out of a courier cost.

4. **WAL-5: COD credit mode is an ENUM, one at a time** (`wallet.cod_credit_mode`: SETTLEMENT | INSTANT_PAY), so "only one enabled" is unrepresentable rather than validated. SETTLEMENT (default) credits when `CourierSettlementService.record` lands the courier's payout — delivery is NOT the trigger, because crediting there fronts 5-10 days of every seller's COD. INSTANT_PAY credits at DELIVERED for a per-seller fee charged on the POST-GST amount (the tax was never theirs to be advanced); `WalletEntryDirection.INSTANT_PAY_FEE` is its own value so the revenue is countable. **AMENDED 2026-09-12 (owner): the COD fee and the Instant Pay fee are INDEPENDENT charges, each off at 0%.** `wallet.cod_collection_fee_percent` is taken on EVERY COD credit, SETTLEMENT or INSTANT_PAY, as its own `COD_COLLECTION_FEE` entry; `wallet.instant_pay_fee_percent` is taken only on an INSTANT_PAY credit, as its own `INSTANT_PAY_FEE` entry, IN ADDITION to the COD fee — it is no longer all-in, and the higher-of-the-two rule is gone. Both on the POST-GST amount, each rounded to the paisa on its own, and a 0% fee writes no entry. ₹1,180 COD: tax ₹180, post-GST ₹1,000, 1% COD fee ₹10, 2.5% Instant Pay ₹25 — credited ₹965 on Instant Pay, ₹990 on settlement. A reversal returns each fee once (`COD_DEDUCTION_REFUND` via `linkedEntryId`). The two fees together never exceed the post-GST amount: the Instant Pay fee is capped at what the COD fee left, and a settings pair summing past 100% is refused on write (`COD_FEES_EXCEED_100`; a global change is checked against the other global value only, so the cap is the backstop for an existing seller override).

5. **WAL-6: a seller is credited what the ORDER WAS WORTH, not what the courier remitted.** A short payment is our dispute with the courier — the seller has no visibility into them and no leverage, and carrying that is the service. **This deliberately REVERSES the original SETL-1** ("the settlement ledger never writes a wallet entry"), which was written when sellers were paid BEFORE settlement and was about not clawing back. The circuit breaker is `wallet.settlement_shortfall_alert_percent`: a payout short by more than that audits CRITICAL and asks for a human, so we absorb the occasional error without quietly funding a systematic one. **AMENDED 2026-09-11 — a REVERSED COD is taken back, exactly.** A short-payment is ours to absorb; a reversal is different — the courier paid out on a parcel that then came back, so the customer never paid and the seller must not keep the credit. A payout carrying an RTO reversal must NAME the orders (`deductions.rtoReversals`; a total with none is `SETTLEMENT_RTO_REVERSAL_ORDERS_REQUIRED`, a mismatch against the file's total `…_TOTAL_MISMATCH`). **Each reversal is a NEGATIVE payout line** (`settled_inr = −R`), and R must equal what the courier has paid on that order net so far — the courier takes back what it paid, so a short-paid order's reversal is its settled amount, not its COD (`…_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`; never paid on a recorded payout is `…_NOT_PAID`; an order both paid and reversed in one payout is refused). "Fully explained" is `amount + early-COD fee + freight = allocated`, the reversal sitting inside `allocated`. `CodCreditService.reverseForOrder` debits `COD_REVERSAL` for the latest UNREVERSED credit in full and credits one `COD_DEDUCTION_REFUND` per tax / fee entry not already returned, `linkedEntryId` naming the deduction it returns. **An order is credited iff it has more `COD_COLLECTION` than `COD_REVERSAL` entries**, so a COD reversed by mistake and paid again is credited again (the `gst_withholdings` row is upserted — it is read nowhere). The reversal's cash leaves as the SELLER's up to what they hold across ALL their INR accounts, CAPITAL beyond it (their wallet then shows the debt — TRE-8's receivable). **A seller is held a COD in the bank book only when THIS payout credits it**, and that cash is posted BEFORE the credit — the tax and fee the credit deducts are reclassified against cash that is already there; posted after, the clamp found nothing and the deductions stayed "the seller's" (₹608.64 on production, 2026-09-12). The rest of a part-payment repays what we fronted and is capital's. Prior payments are read inside the transaction under a per-order advisory lock (`AdvisoryLock.SETTLEMENT_ORDER`, taken in sorted order), so two payouts for one order cannot both see "nothing paid yet". A line or reversal whose order was carried by a different courier account is refused (`SETTLEMENT_ORDER_OTHER_COURIER`). **Instant Pay fronts the COD from capital at delivery** — a capital→seller pair in the courier's payout account before the credit — so the credit has cash behind it and the payout that later reimburses it is capital's.

6. **Every new `WalletEntryDirection` MUST be registered in `WalletService.CREDIT_DIRECTIONS` AND routed in `isWalletCredit` / `walletDirectionLabel` in `@skydrop/ui/status`** (WAL-1). TOPUP, ORDER_CHARGES_REFUND, COD_DEDUCTION_REFUND and INBOUND_FREIGHT_REFUND are CREDITS; RTO_FEE, INBOUND_FREIGHT, CUSTOMER_RETURN_FEE, INSTANT_PAY_FEE, COD_COLLECTION_FEE, GST_WITHHOLDING and COD_REVERSAL are DEBITS. **AMENDED 2026-08-31: the UI half is no longer a hand-maintained Set copied into apps/seller.** It is ONE exhaustive switch in `@skydrop/ui/status`, read by apps/seller AND apps/admin, so a new direction fails to BUILD until somebody decides which way it points — the failure mode the old Set had (silently defaulting to debit, i.e. taking money instead of giving it) is now unrepresentable. `order-charges-refund.service.spec.ts` compares the API Set and the UI switch AS WHOLE SETS in both directions; the previous check asserted a single value appeared in both files, which passes while the two disagree about any OTHER direction. **Do not reintroduce a per-app credit set** — that is the third copy the whole invariant exists to prevent.

7. **WAL-7 (2026-08-02): every write to one wallet is SERIALIZED, and the balance is the LAST ENTRY's running balance — never a re-sum.** `applyEntry` takes `pg_advisory_xact_lock(AdvisoryLock.WALLET, hash(sellerId|currency))` before reading. Without it, two concurrent credits both read the same balance and stamp the same `runningBalanceAfter`; the ledger is append-only, so the error is permanent. This was REAL, not theoretical — 12 concurrent ₹10 credits produced a final running balance of ₹20, and a mixed run came out at −8 instead of 36. `wallet-concurrency.e2e-spec.ts` is the regression guard and FAILS on the unlocked implementation; a mocked Prisma has no concurrency to expose, so only a real database distinguishes them (same argument as the pack-box and pickup-request partial uniques). The balance read is `ORDER BY id DESC LIMIT 1` on `(seller_id, currency, id)` — ids are uuidv7 so they are monotonic and, unlike `createdAt`, distinct within one transaction (Postgres fixes CURRENT_TIMESTAMP per tx, so the COD credit and charges debit written together share a timestamp). `balanceLive` survives as the SQL-summed verification path and `verifyBalance` compares the two, but NEITHER is on the write path: an O(history) check charged to every write, whose result nothing reads, is a cost that grows with how long a seller has traded and is not auditing. **Any future money guard that reads a balance or counts rows before writing must hold this same lock inside the same transaction** — `WithdrawalRequestService.createInternal` does.

7b. **WAL-8 (2026-09-12): the money a delivery owes follows EVERY writer of DELIVERED, not the lifecycle bus.** The ORDER_CHARGES debit, the Instant Pay COD credit and the inbound-freight share hung off one bus subscriber, and only `OrderWriteService.transitionStatus` emits on the bus — god mode (ORD-2) writes `orders.status` itself, so an order it forced to DELIVERED was carried free until the backfill found it. `DeliveredAccrualService.accrueForDelivered` is the one dispatch (tier → execute or schedule), and `OrderDeliveredAccrualListener` is its ONLY caller: since 2026-09-12 god mode emits the same lifecycle event as a matrix transition (ORD-2), so both writers of DELIVERED reach the money through one path. A failure audits HIGH `wallet.delivered_accrual_failed` with its `trigger` (`force_mutation` / `lifecycle_transition`) rather than throwing — **and since 2026-09-19 it also RAISES a HIGH MONEY `delivered-accrual-failed:<orderId>`, because nothing reads audit rows and an unbilled delivered order was therefore invisible; every step is idempotent under the WALLET lock, so re-running `handle` is the fix and that success clears it.** The reseller half is the same shape (`reseller-money-step:<orderId>`, `ResellerOrderMoneyListener`). **God mode bills on purpose** — it opts out of stock compensation, not money. Exactly-once rests on the accrual's own in-transaction gates under the WALLET lock. `delivered-money-paths.spec.ts` pins the set of `orders.status` writers AND that nothing but the listener calls the accrual; **a new writer must go through `transitionStatus` or emit the same event.** **AMENDED (review, 2026-09-12) — the money follows the order's FATE.** The accrual bills only an order that is STILL DELIVERED, rechecked inside its transaction under the WALLET lock; the sweep closes a skipped row with `pending_accruals.skipped_reason`, and a cancel, reject or LOSS retires it at once (`EndedOrderMoneyService`, from the shared post-commit hooks) — a god-mode DELIVERED then cancel used to be billed seven days later on a cancelled order. Billing asks the SAME carriage question as the refund (`parcelLeftWithCourier`, `modules/order/order-carriage.ts`): with no carriage evidence the delivery charge is still billed (a later call-off refunds it on the same evidence), but the Instant Pay credit and the freight share are WITHHELD and a HIGH MONEY issue `delivered-without-carriage:<orderId>` is raised. An uncovered Instant Pay credit on a called-off, never-carried order is reversed (`reverseForOrder`) and the fronted cash returns to capital (`takeToCapital`, the settlement's `max(0,before) − max(0,before−gross)`; the pair references the reversal entry, never the order id, which WAL-9 reserves for the front). **LOST_IN_TRANSIT refunds ORDER_CHARGES** (the owner's rule: a lost parcel is not charged); charges and refunds PAIR UP in both the debit gate and the refund, and a skipped accrual row is re-armed on re-delivery. **AMENDED 2026-09-27: lost-then-found does NOT re-bill, and the sentence that said it did was describing an impossibility.** The pairing gate reaches "owed again" with one charge and one refund, and `seller_wallet_entries_once_per_order_uq` covers `(linked_order_id, direction)` for nine directions INCLUDING `order_charges` — so the second write raised P2002 with nothing catching it, aborting the WHOLE delivered-money transaction (the Instant Pay front and credit and the freight share roll back with it) and leaving `delivered-accrual-failed:<orderId>` un-clearable, because its own remedy is to re-run `handle` and every re-run hit the identical violation. **The INDEX wins** — it is the guard against paying an order twice and is not weakened — and a P2002 aborts the transaction, so the conflict cannot be caught and carried on from either: `debitIfNeeded` asks FIRST whether a charge already exists, skips it, lets the COD credit and the freight share commit, and raises HIGH MONEY `order-charges-not-rebillable:<orderId>` naming the order and saying to take the fee by hand on /wallet-transfers (TRE-8b exists for exactly this). It never auto-clears, because nothing will ever make it true. **`rto_fee` / `customer_return_fee` / `order_charges_refund` are UNAFFECTED** and were checked: the return fee gates on a plain existence query, which is what the index enforces, and the refund can never see two charges to pair against because the index caps them at one. **`cod_collection` had the SAME contradiction and WAS FIXED on 2026-10-13 (owner), the opposite way round** — see WAL-10 below. The difference is which of the two is right: a delivery fee genuinely happens once, so the index is correct and the code skips; a COD reversed and paid again IS credited again (WAL-6), so the index was wrong and it moved. **Do not read the `order_charges` decision as a precedent for a direction whose business rule says it recurs**, nor WAL-10 as a precedent for one that does not. Proved against a real database by `order-charges-rebill.e2e-spec.ts`; **a mocked Prisma has no index, and a mocked test of the old gate passed while Postgres was refusing what it asserted.** **Known gap:** there is no reversal for a freight share already charged before this rule (it is now never charged without carriage evidence). **A script that writes order rows directly skips all of it too** — the production test parcels seeded from Shiprocket on 2026-09-11 were billed by the backfill hours later.

7b-ii. **WAL-10 (2026-10-13, owner): a COD credit may RECUR, and the index that said otherwise was the thing that was wrong.** `seller_wallet_entries_once_per_order_uq` covered nine directions; four of them — `cod_collection`, `gst_withholding`, `cod_collection_fee`, `instant_pay_fee` — are the COD-credit family, and WAL-6 states plainly that a COD reversed by mistake and paid again is credited again. The index forbade exactly that, so the database and the business rule contradicted each other, and the contradiction did not surface as a tidy refusal: a re-credit needs a second row of each, the P2002 aborts the surrounding transaction, and for a courier payout that transaction is the settlement row, every line, **every OTHER seller's credit on that payout, and the bank entry for cash that really landed**. Re-uploading the file reproduced it exactly, so one order could stop a payout covering sixty with no way through from any screen. It fired on the default configuration (`wallet.cod_gst_percent` 18). **The index now covers FIVE** (`customer_return_fee`, `inbound_freight`, `order_charges`, `order_charges_refund`, `rto_fee`), each of which is separately guarded by a plain existence or pairing query — the same thing a unique index enforces — so both layers still hold for them. **What guards the four instead**: `AdvisoryLock.WALLET`, taken by `WalletService.applyEntry` itself (it takes a transaction client as its first argument, so it cannot be called outside one), then the counting gate read under that lock in the same transaction — `CodCreditService.isCredited`, credits greater than reversals — or, on a reseller order, a guarded `updateMany` on `reseller_order_credits.status = DUE`. The three deductions are written inside the same gated branch as the credit, so they cannot drift from it, and `wallet-guard-locking.spec.ts` fails CI per METHOD if a future writer reads the ledger before locking. **Say what was lost rather than discover it later: this is a database guarantee traded for a lock-plus-CI guarantee, weaker in kind.** **The change REQUIRED a second one, and that is the part worth remembering.** `reverseForOrder` paired credits to reversals exactly (`linkedEntryId`) but refunded **every unrefunded deduction on the ORDER** — correct only while the index made a second unrefunded set unrepresentable. With a re-credit permitted, one reversal would have returned two credits' worth of tax and fees. That error is WORSE than the double credit the index guarded: an inflated balance shows up in a reconciliation, while an over-refund leaves the wallet internally consistent and silently understates the P&L's COD tax and COD handling fee lines. The deductions now carry `linkedEntryId` to the COD they were taken from and the refund narrows on it, falling back to the order-wide query only for rows written before this (which carry null, and for which the old index guarantees there was only ever one set). **Before relaxing a uniqueness constraint, ask what ELSE was relying on it** — here a query three hundred lines away, whose correctness nothing in its own file disclosed. **Deliberately NOT changed: `order_charges`** (a delivery fee genuinely happens once; the 2026-09-27 skip-and-raise stands, WAL-8) and **the reseller re-credit refusal** — `ResellerOrderMoneyService.refusedDeductions` is an existence check, not a counting gate, so a reseller order whose COD is re-paid is still skipped and named rather than re-credited. That is conservative and safe; making it recur has to agree with `reseller_order_credits` and the RS-7 dispute path, which is a separate decision.

7c. **WAL-9 (2026-09-12): what Instant Pay has ADVANCED is derived per order and is a named part of the courier float.** At delivery an Instant Pay seller is credited the COD less GST, the COD fee, the Instant Pay fee and the delivery charge, all at that moment; the cash behind it is OUR money in the courier's payout account moved to the seller (`SellerCashAttributionService.front`, a capital→seller pair — NOT capped at what capital holds there, so our share of an account may go negative until the courier pays), less any part of the COD that repaid a debt. When the courier pays, the whole COD is capital's: it clears the advance and brings in the charges. `InstantPayAdvanceService` lists every order DELIVERED with a COD, NO `courier_settlement_lines` row, and more `COD_COLLECTION` than `COD_REVERSAL` entries — structural, because a settlement-mode credit is written inside the payout that pays the order, so only Instant Pay credits a COD with no payout behind it. Never test for `INSTANT_PAY_FEE` (absent at 0%) or the front pair (absent when the whole COD repaid a debt), and never read the credit's note. The cash fronted is the SELLER half of the pair `front` posts with `reference = orderId` — the ONLY pair referenced by an order id; keep it that way or the front is miscounted. The liabilities `courier_float` line keeps its key and total and carries two parts (`courier_float_instant_pay`, `courier_float_settlement`) that sum to it exactly (same no-payout-line predicate; settlement is the remainder); parts are never added to a total. `GET /admin/treasury/instant-pay-advances` (`money.treasury.view`), drill-down at `/liabilities/instant-pay`, and a per-seller figure on the seller wallet page. Nothing stored. An order qualifies if it EVER reached DELIVERED (`order_events`), not only if it is DELIVERED now — a fronted Instant Pay COD whose parcel came back before the courier paid was dropping out of both the list and the float; a settlement-mode order returned after delivery is not added back (nothing was credited or fronted on it).

8. **Advisory locks live in `common/db/advisory-lock.ts`.** The namespace registry and the FNV-1a hash are declared once; three subsystems had already copy-pasted the hash and documented their namespaces in scattered comments, which is how two of them eventually pick the same number and start waiting on each other for no reason. Add a new namespace there, not as a local constant.
