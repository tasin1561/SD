**Treasury & bank-book rules (TRE-1 through TRE-7, 2026-08-28 — NON-NEGOTIABLE):**

1. **TRE-1: `BankLedgerService.post()` is the sole writer of `bank_entries`, and a balance is SUMMED from them, never cached.** No exceptions — `SellerCashAttributionService` posts its reclassification PAIR through it rather than inserting, because post() is where the account is checked for existence and retirement, where the currency is checked against it, and where a capital row carrying a seller is refused; a direct insert inherits none of that and the first thing it lets through is cash posted into a retired account. **Anything that READS a balance and then writes based on it holds `AdvisoryLock.BANK_RECONCILE` inside the same transaction** — `reconcile()` shipped without that and two operators correcting one account both read the same figure and both posted the same difference, permanently, because the ledger is append-only. **An account holding money cannot be retired** (`BANK_ACCOUNT_NOT_EMPTY`): the overview iterates non-deleted accounts but groups over all entries, so retiring a funded one drops the money from every per-account figure while the client-money total still counts it. Every account's total, and the split between what is ours and what is held for a seller, is a `groupBy` over the entries. The wallet cached its balance and left the refresh to each caller; six of fourteen money paths remembered, and a seller owing ₹3,000 read as ₹0.00. Money read wrong is worse than money read slowly.

2. **TRE-2: every caller states `amountCurrency`, and a mismatch is REFUSED.** The entry is stamped with the ACCOUNT's currency whatever arrives, so a caller handing over BDT for an INR account would not fail — it would be relabelled, wrong by a factor of the exchange rate, with nothing in the row to show it happened. Required rather than optional, so the mistake is unrepresentable rather than merely unlikely.

3. **TRE-3: a flow that moves money writes the cash side in the SAME transaction as the business event.** Courier settlement, top-up acceptance and remittance all post their bank entry inside the tx that writes the wallet entry. A bank line that can commit without its cause, or a cause without its line, is how a statement stops matching the story. A settlement with no bank account linked to its courier account is REFUSED (`SETTLEMENT_NO_RECEIVING_ACCOUNT`) — a settlement whose cash was never recorded reads on the coverage page as money we hold and do not.

4. **TRE-4: settlement attribution holds each seller what we CREDITED them, and capital carries the difference.** The remainder is positive when the courier reimburses instant-pay orders we already funded and negative when they short-paid and we absorbed it (WAL-6). That split keeps seller-held cash equal to wallet liability and leaves the dispute sitting visibly against our own money rather than quietly shrinking somebody's balance.

5. **TRE-5: a cross-currency transfer credits the seller at the QUOTED rate; the gap against what we achieved is `FX_SPREAD`, ours, either way.** Both amounts are entered from the two statements — deriving the second from a rate would silently absorb every bank charge. Honouring a quote the market moved against is a real cost and is recorded as one, which is also why FX is a P&L line rather than an adjustment.

6. **TRE-6: the P&L states how much of its cost side is MEASURED.** Four sources kept apart (BD→India freight, India delivery, returns, FX) because they have different fixes. Costs are nullable and a missing one is reported as uncovered, NEVER defaulted — zero would report the whole of that revenue as profit, and the estimate would hide how much of the report is guesswork. **Forward and return costs are SEPARATE columns** (`actual_courier_cost_inr` / `actual_rto_cost_inr`), and **every P&L line reads a parcel's cost as the two ADDED** (AMENDED 2026-09-11). The lines stay disjoint on `rtoReceivedAt` — delivery takes parcels not received back, returns takes those received — so nothing is counted twice. The old rule (delivery reads forward only, returns reads return only) existed because the importer never applied Delhivery's refund of the delivery charge on a return, so adding the columns counted that carriage twice. COST-1 nets the refund, which leaves a returned parcel at ₹0 forward and makes the sum the truth; reading one column alone then LOST cost twice over: a parcel turned round but not yet received (or lost on the way back) read as free, and a manual courier's forward charge on a return vanished from every line. Nothing is stored — the report derives on read from append-only ledgers. **AMENDED 2026-09-11 — every rupee Skydrop earns or spends is on a line, in RUPEES.** An audit of every wallet direction and bank entry type found money on no line at all; now: **Returns** earns the returned parcel's delivery fee AND its return fee (both order-charge lines on the returned order — it read ₹30 against a whole round trip); **delivery** subtracts `ORDER_CHARGES_REFUND` on orders still in its cohort and counts GST charged on our fees (we file no return against it — the founder, as with the COD deduction); a **WAIVED** freight bill earns only `amount_settled_inr`; **COD handling fees** (`INSTANT_PAY_FEE` + `COD_COLLECTION_FEE`), **damage & loss refunds** (`SCRAP_REFUND` — the courier's lost-shipment credit was counted while our payout to the seller was not, so a loss read as a gain), **courier charges on no live Skydrop parcel** (PARCEL transactions whose waybill matches no live shipment — a voided parcel's charges fell out of every line; counted from the same cutover as adjustments), **bank reconciliation differences** on CAPITAL and **investment income** (returned − placed, at close) each have their own line. **Nothing in another currency is added as rupees**: an FX spread converts at its own transfer's rate, a taka expense at the rate in force that day (`fx_rate_history`, else `fx_rates`); an amount with no rate is LEFT OUT and named in `report.warnings`, and `complete` goes false. When adding a money flow, add its line in the same change — a flow the P&L cannot see reads as profit. **AMENDED again 2026-09-11 — what the courier short-pays or takes back.** **COD short-payments absorbed** is a cost line: each payout line stores `shortfall_inr` = the order's TARGET shortfall after the line minus what its earlier lines already recognised, where the target is `max(0, expected − net paid)` and ZERO on a reversal line (the credit is gone, so is the loss). A later payout that makes a gap up comes back off as a negative, a two-part payment nets to nothing, and reversing a short-paid order recovers the gap it had absorbed — computed from `expected − settled` per line it would book the first part's gap and never the recovery. A deduction returned on a REVERSED COD (`COD_DEDUCTION_REFUND`) comes off the line that counted it — tax off **COD tax**, a fee off **COD handling fees** — read through the deduction its `linkedEntryId` names, never guessed from its note. **An OPENING BALANCE is not income**: a capital `RECONCILIATION_ADJUSTMENT` that is the account's FIRST entry (no earlier id on that account — `reconcile()` is the only way to post one) is money the business already had, and it is left off the bank-reconciliation line and named in its note. It shipped counted for a day: a ৳100,000 "Initial Balance" read as ₹81,300.81 of profit. **AMENDED 2026-09-12 — owner money, and a parcel's FATE.** Money the owner puts in or takes out later is `OWNER_CONTRIBUTION` / `OWNER_DRAWING` (capital, the account's currency, `POST /admin/treasury/accounts/:id/owner-money`, audited HIGH) and the P&L never reads either — reconciling it in would have booked it as income or a loss. The opening-balance rule reads the account's first CAPITAL entry. A same-currency transfer that arrives short books the difference as a `bank_charges` EXPENSE rather than letting it vanish. Investment income converts at its close date's rate. **Delivery and returns are recognised when the parcel's fate is known, not when it was booked**: delivery = orders that first reached `DELIVERED` (or `LOST_IN_TRANSIT`) in the window, read from `order_events` because nothing writes `shipments.delivered_at`; returns = received back in the window; superseded shipments are in neither; a delivered-then-returned order is on returns alone; parcels still with the courier are on no line and counted in the delivery note. Booked-at-creation put ₹3,800 of production's ₹6,000 "delivery revenue" on parcels still moving or cancelled. Every drill-down uses exactly its total's filters. Courier charges on a VOIDED or superseded Skydrop parcel count regardless of the cutover (they are ours); charges matching no parcel count from the cutover; "ours" requires the same courier as the charging account. A past amount converted at today's rate (no history for its day) is named in `report.warnings`. **AMENDED again 2026-09-12 — every line TILES and every drill-down ADDS UP, by construction.** A second audit found totals and drill-downs computed two different ways (four lines had no drill-down at all, FX rows were taka printed as rupees, refund rows were missing, and the delivery rows skipped an order with no waybill while coverage still read "fully priced"). Delivery, returns, FX, unmatched courier charges, bank reconciliation and investment income now read ONE shared computation for both, and every other line uses its total's exact filters. **Windows are half-open `[from, to)`** — every query is `lt: to`, and the admin sends the NEXT IST midnight; the old `lte 23:59:59.999` left a microsecond timestamp in no window. **An amount converts at the latest rate recorded at or before its OWN instant**, never a per-day cached one. **Returns are the order's FATE too**: its first `RTO_RECEIVED` (or `RTO_RESTOCKED` when receive was skipped) `order_event` in the window — not `shipments.rto_received_at` — and returns subtract `ORDER_CHARGES_REFUND` as delivery does. **A LOST parcel earns nothing** (the founder, 2026-09-12: a lost parcel is not charged): its courier cost stays on the delivery line, labelled, with no revenue. **Drill-down rows are per ORDER** — charges once, cost summed over its live shipments — and an order with no waybill-bearing shipment is UNCOVERED, never priced. A charge on a live shipment's `reverseAwbNumber` is ours. `pnl.service.spec.ts` runs against `test/unit/pnl-fake-db.ts`, an in-memory Prisma that APPLIES the where-clauses: the old fake answered every query alike, which is exactly why it could not see a window or a drill-down disagree. It pins, for every line, that two adjacent windows sum to the whole and that the rows sum to the total — **a new line must join both tests**. **AMENDED again 2026-09-12 (third audit) — one date per payout, called-off orders, every charge type.** **Everything a courier payout produces is dated by when it was RECORDED** (`created_at` of the settlement line, the wallet entry and the early-COD bank entry — one transaction, one instant), never by its typed `received_at`: wallet deductions can be dated no other way (an Instant Pay deduction has no payout), a typed date can be back-dated into a closed month, and an `allocateMore` line is recognised when it is added, with its credit. **An order CALLED OFF (cancelled or rejected) with money on it is on the delivery line**, labelled, dated by its LAST event into a called-off status; revenue is the ORDER_CHARGES debit net of refunds (on AT_DELIVERY a cancelled order was never billed), cost from LIVE shipments only (a parcel voided at cancel is on the no-live-parcel line). `orderFate()` places every `OrderStatus` (delivered / returned / called_off / open), F2-exhaustive and pinned against the state machine's terminals; an OPEN order is on no line and the delivery note counts its waybilled parcels and the courier cost already on them — a waybill is charged at confirmation, so a PICKED parcel can carry cost. **`chargeLeg()` places every `ChargeType`**: all but RTO_FEE and REFUND are delivery revenue, returns add RTO_FEE, REFUND is excluded as the accrual excludes it. **A courier adjustment naming a Skydrop parcel counts before the cutover** (the no-live-parcel line's matcher). A payout's unallocated excess is NAMED in the short-payments note, never counted. Fate-based restatements (delivered then returned, lost then found) are SAID in the notes. `from`/`to`/`limit` are validated (`INVALID_DATE` / `INVALID_RANGE` / `INVALID_LIMIT`). **AMENDED again 2026-09-12 (fourth review) — billed, current fate, one matcher.** Delivery, returns, lost and called-off revenue is what was DEBITED on the order (ORDER_CHARGES + RTO_FEE + CUSTOMER_RETURN_FEE) less ORDER_CHARGES_REFUND — never the charge lines, which are a quote until billed; a line no debit covers is named in the note, not counted. A LOST parcel earns the fee still held on it (₹0 once refunded — the owner's rule is that a lost parcel is not charged) and the note says whether it is held, refunded or never billed. Every cohort's statuses come from `orderFate` (`statusesOf`) — RTO_DAMAGED had been on no line. An order is counted only in the fate it is in NOW (`currentFate`), dated by the first event of its final unbroken stretch in that fate (`walkFate`); RTO_INITIATED/RTO_IN_TRANSIT after a delivery continue the delivered stretch (a customer return stays on delivery until received; one lost on the way back is labelled so). A delivered order forced to cancelled is called off, dated by the cancellation. The open-parcels note is as of the window's END. `parcelMatcher` answers with the shipment the importer nets onto (own waybill first — even a voided one — then a waybill another shipment holds is nobody's alias, then return waybill, then order id, live preferred; same courier, account or none), so every charge lands on exactly one line. A forwarder payment priced at today's rate makes the report warn and `complete` false.

6b. **PNL-CF-1 (2026-09-14): the carry-forward P&L — a month is FROZEN once it closes, NEVER reopened, and every later change is carried into the month then open.** Owner: costs and charges keep moving sixty days after dispatch, and a reported month must stop moving. Leaf module `pnl-carry-forward`, page `/pnl/carry-forward`; /pnl itself is unchanged. **Close** freezes `PnlService.report()` for `[1st 00:00 IST, next 1st)` — the SAME engine call /pnl makes — into `pnl_periods.report`, and every drill-down row into `pnl_snapshot_rows`, keyed by the record's STABLE id: `PnlLineItem.id` (the order for delivery/returns, a bank or wallet entry, a settlement line, an investment, `<courier account>|<txn id>` for adjustments, `<courier account>|<awb>` for unmatched charges). Operating expenses are frozen as a line of their own (`PNL_SNAPSHOT_KEYS`; `expenses()` now sums `expenseRows`, so figure and rows are one computation). A close is REFUSED (`PNL_ROWS_DISAGREE`) when a line's rows do not add up to its figure — that month could never reconcile. **Detect** recomputes every closed month with the live engine and diffs it record by record against snapshot + every carry-forward already written (`diffMonth`, pure); each non-zero revenue or cost movement is an APPEND-ONLY `pnl_carry_forwards` row with before, after and a reason ("Courier cost not recorded at close (uncovered) → ₹104.38", "Order SD-B has left India delivery since the month closed — it came back to us", "Expense dated 28 Aug 2026 recorded after the month closed"), counted in `landed_month`, the month OPEN when it was found. Running it twice adds nothing: the baseline already includes the first run. **No month is REOPENED — it is re-LOCKED, and every lock is a VERSION (AMENDED 2026-09-14, owner: "auto lock the month if any nightly run didn't work and then raise an issue so that I can solve that run and then override the last auto lock with any missing data and lock it permanently").** A month has a lock state, `pnl_periods.lock_state` PROVISIONAL | FINAL, and one or more `pnl_snapshot_versions` (kind AUTO_FINAL / AUTO_PROVISIONAL / LOCK_PERMANENTLY / GOD_MODE / BACKFILL / MANUAL; the current one has no `superseded_at`); snapshot rows belong to a version, and no version or row is ever deleted or overwritten — a replaced version only gets `superseded_at`, and any version opens read-only (`?version=N`). **PROVISIONAL**: the scheduled close happens on time whatever the nightly jobs did; when one failed, was switched off or did not run, the month closes PROVISIONAL and a HIGH MONEY issue `pnl-close-provisional:<YYYY-MM>` names each job (the gate's own reasons) and says what to do. **Detection SKIPS a provisional month** — nothing is carried out of it, its late data stays IN it. **Lock permanently** (`POST .../pnl-periods/:month/lock-permanently`, `money.pnl.close`, reason ≥ 10, HIGH) re-snapshots it with the live engine NOW as a new FINAL version, is allowed even when the gate still fails (the owner decides; the audit says `lockedDespiteNightlyJobs`), resolves the issue, and detection resumes for it. **God mode** (`POST .../pnl-periods/:month/god-mode-relock`, `money.pnl.god_mode` — its own dangerous permission, SUPER_ADMIN by construction; reason ≥ 30, `confirmMonth` typed exactly as the month, `acknowledgeRisk: true`; audited CRITICAL, `entityId` null, month in metadata; refused for a month not ended or not closed) re-locks a FINAL month (on a PROVISIONAL one it is lock-permanently under the stricter guardrails). **The re-lock formula, per record and per line: new version = live(month) − Σ carry-forwards ALREADY recorded against that month** (`rebaseRows`, `subtractCarriedFromReport`, pure). Carry-forward rows are append-only and stay in the months they landed in, so the re-locked month holds only what no later month has already reported: a record that left the month keeps a row of minus what was carried, each row keeps its full figure at the lock in `label.atLock` (the "before" of the next change), and carry-forwards found BEFORE the current version count numerically only (`buildBaseline(…, rebasedAt)`). Detection and re-lock both compute the live month UNDER `AdvisoryLock.PNL_PERIOD`, so no carry-forward is written between reading the month and subtracting what was carried. A month earlier than a closed one still cannot be closed (`PNL_LATER_MONTH_CLOSED`), nor one not yet ended (`PNL_MONTH_NOT_ENDED`); a carry-forward into a closed month is refused (`PNL_MONTH_CLOSED`). **Just before a month closes, changes to the months before it are carried INTO it**, so a closed month's view — its snapshot plus what landed in it — is everything reported up to the moment it closed, and stays that way. **Schedule** (`PnlPeriodWorker`, queue `pnl-period`, SCALE-1): `pnl-auto-close` hourly (`Asia/Kolkata`) acts from 06:00 IST on the 1st on every ended month after the last closed one, oldest first, closing it FINAL when `PnlNightlyGateService` finds that the LATEST run since the month ended of each nightly job — Delhivery wallet sync, Shiprocket wallet sync, Delhivery and Shiprocket invoice checks — succeeded, read from the jobs' own audit rows (`courier.wallet_ledger.synced`, `courier.shiprocket_wallet.synced`, `courier.delhivery_invoices.checked`, `courier.shiprocket_invoices.checked`), and PROVISIONAL otherwise: a `*_failed` row, `skipped: 'DISABLED'`, no run, or any account with an `error` or an `outcome` other than READ / CHECKED / SKIPPED (a manual re-run counts; `NO_ACCOUNTS` passes). The old "wait and retry hourly" and its `pnl-close:<YYYY-MM>` issue are RETIRED (resolved on any close); only a close that throws is retried hourly, as HIGH `pnl-close-failed:<YYYY-MM>`. With no month ever closed it closes nothing and raises `pnl-close:never-closed`. `pnl-detect-carry-forwards` runs 06:15 IST daily. **By hand** (`money.pnl.close`, dangerous, SUPER_ADMIN by construction): `POST /admin/treasury/pnl-periods/:month/close` (FINAL, reason ≥ 10 chars) and `POST /admin/treasury/pnl-periods/backfill-close` (FINAL; DRY RUN unless `dryRun: false` + a reason; closes every month from the first with P&L activity through `throughMonth`, default last month — run once after deploy, never a migration, because a snapshot needs the engine). Audited HIGH, `entityId: null`, the month in metadata. `20260914100000_pnl_lock_states_and_versions` made every month already closed FINAL and its snapshot version 1. **The invariant**, pinned by `pnl-carry-forward.spec.ts` on `pnl-fake-db.ts` (including after a god-mode re-lock, and after a change found once the re-lock is done): each FINAL month's current version plus its carry-forwards equals the month recomputed, and over any span the current versions plus every carry-forward plus the open month's own figure equal the live engine over the span, per line, to the paisa. A PROVISIONAL month is outside it until it is locked permanently — by design, its late data is waiting to go into it. **A new P&L line must give every drill-down row a stable `id`**, or it cannot be frozen or compared. Detection re-runs every closed month daily, so it grows with the months kept; cap its look-back if that ever becomes slow.

7. **TRE-7: a negative wallet is bounded by a flat limit PLUS the cost value of the seller's stock, and the block is at ORDER CREATE.** Their goods are in our warehouse, so slack is correct; unbounded slack is not, because every further order spends money we are already owed. Create is the last point where nothing is committed — at confirmation an agent finds out with the customer on the line, at dispatch the goods are already picked. It is NOT a `seller_restrictions` row (that table is for deliberate staff holds, one-active-per-seller, lifted by a person); a credit block is computed and self-correcting. **Resolution FAILS OPEN**, and stock is valued at COST with an unknown unit cost contributing nothing.

8. **TRE-8: seller money is EXACTLY two things — what they topped up, and what the courier collected on their behalf. Everything else in the account is capital.** A top-up and a COD credit bring real cash in and it is theirs; a remittance takes real cash out. But a CHARGE moves nothing between banks — it changes whose the cash already sitting there is, so `SellerCashAttributionService` posts a PAIR summing to zero (negative SELLER, positive CAPITAL) inside the wallet's own transaction. A pair, never one entry: the cash does not leave, so a single row would change the account total and make it disagree with the statement. Inbound freight is the motivating case — we pay the forwarder out of capital and recover it INTO capital as parcels deliver. **The reclassification is CLAMPED to what the seller actually holds, so a seller with no cash with us produces NO bank entry at all** — a negative wallet has nothing behind it in any account and is a RECEIVABLE, not cash; inventing an entry would put a number in the book no statement will ever agree with. Which directions move cash and which way is an F2-exhaustive switch (a new `WalletEntryDirection` fails to compile until someone decides); operator adjustments deliberately move nothing, because whether cash is implicated is exactly what is being corrected. The debt's CAUSES are read from the point the balance last stood at or above zero — over the whole history an old account would look far worse than a new one. **AMENDED 2026-09-12 — the other half of the receivable: cash that REPAYS it is ours.** A charge on a seller holding nothing correctly writes no bank entry; but when their COD or top-up then arrived, the whole inflow was posted as theirs, so the part that paid the debt off stayed "held for the seller" forever (₹3,600 on Menev Store in production). Every cash-in now splits first (`SellerCashAttributionService.debtSplit`): `min(debt, amount)` to CAPITAL, only the rest to the seller — a taka top-up in proportion — which is what keeps seller-held cash equal to `max(0, wallet)`. `settlement-bank-invariant.spec.ts` runs the real credit and attribution services over an in-memory book and asserts exactly that after every scenario; a new cash-in path must be added to it. **AMENDED again 2026-09-12 (second audit) — the invariant holds across CURRENCIES, refunds, reversals and payouts.** (a) **A seller's holding is every account, taka included**: a charge (and the debt split, and a settlement clawback) takes their rupees first and then their other-currency cash, valued at the rate their money there was CREDITED at — the latest accepted top-up into that account — falling back to today's rate only when no top-up ever landed, and saying which in the pair's note. Stopping at rupees left a taka top-up "theirs" forever while the wallet spent it; that is how Menev Store's ৳4,262 drifted. A seller transfer across currencies with no quote uses the same rate as the quote, so the gap is `FX_SPREAD` (TRE-5); with no rate at all it is refused (`TRANSFER_QUOTE_REQUIRED`). (b) **A refund respects debt**: only `max(0, after) − max(0, before)` moves back to the seller; the rest repaid a receivable and is ours. (c) **Lock order is WALLET then BANK_RECONCILE** everywhere a seller's cash is read and moved — reclassify and seller transfers took only the second, so a correction could race a charge. (d) **A remittance takes the wallet lock BEFORE reading the balance** (WAL-7) and may carry a `bankFee`, booked as our `bank_charges` expense. When the paying account holds less of the seller's money than the payout (the normal BD case — COD sits in rupees at HDFC, the payout leaves in taka from Tasin City), the seller's part is capped at what they hold THERE, the rest leaves as ours, and an equal value of their money elsewhere becomes ours — so the book falls by exactly what the wallet did; refusing would have blocked every BD payout. (e) **A reversal's clawback is capped at the reversed credit's GROSS**, not at what the courier took back: ₹1,000 credited, ₹950 paid and reversed leaves the seller at ₹0 held / ₹0 wallet, and the ₹50 we absorbed comes back to capital. (f) **An uncredited order is credited on its payout whatever the seller's CURRENT mode**, as SETTLEMENT — switching to Instant Pay between delivery and payout used to strand it. **AMENDED again 2026-09-12 (third audit) — BOOK VALUE, not a rate.** Every SELLER `bank_entries` row in a non-rupee account carries `inr_book_value`, what it is worth to their wallet (a top-up: the rupees credited; a charge: minus the rupees charged). Summed per account it IS their wallet's value there, and book ÷ units is their average rate. A charge takes units at that average and book by exactly the rupees; spending a holding takes every unit and every rupee. `BankLedgerService.post()` values any SELLER row a caller did not (outflow at the average, inflow at today's rate), so no path leaves one unvalued. Valued at "their latest top-up's rate" (the rule above, now replaced), ৳1,000@0.70 + ৳1,000@0.80 read ₹1,600 against a ₹1,500 wallet. A seller transfer with NO quote into taka credits what arrived carrying the book that left — no FX_SPREAD (the implied quote booked phantom FX); into rupees it credits the book value and the gap is real FX. A remittance takes the paying account's money at the average, the rest from their money elsewhere at the payout's own `fxRateSnapshot`, and posts the taka paid vs the value removed as a capital `FX_SPREAD` linked by `remittance_id` (the P&L converts it at that payout's rate). A COD reversal takes `max(0, before) − max(0, before − G)`, so with its deduction refunds it nets to `max(0, before) − max(0, after)`. Payouts take every seller's WALLET lock up front, sorted (two payouts touching the same sellers in opposite orders deadlocked); `allocateMore` re-reads the payout under `AdvisoryLock.SETTLEMENT`. Every attribution pair takes ONE shared `BANK_RECONCILE` key (`ATTRIBUTION_RECONCILE_KEY`) after its WALLET lock and `reconcile()` takes it too — one key, not one per account, or two attributions touching two accounts in opposite orders deadlock; without it a charge landing between reconcile's read and post was folded into the correction. **Lock order (review, 2026-09-12): WALLET (sorted) < the ACCOUNT's reconcile key (`lockAccountsForPosting`, sorted) < `ATTRIBUTION_RECONCILE_KEY`.** Every writer that posts a non-pair row into an account — transfers (both accounts), remittances, settlement record and `allocateMore`, top-up acceptance, reclassify, owner money, forwarder payments, courier-wallet recharges, investments, manual expenses — takes that account's key before any attribution pair; `reconcile()` takes account key → attribution and no WALLET lock; attribution pairs take only the attribution key. Nothing takes a lower-ranked lock after a higher one, so there is no cycle, and a reconcile can no longer fold in money posted between its read and its post. A SELLER row in a non-rupee account posted by `reconcile` or by reclassify TOWARD a seller carries an operator-stated `inrValue` (required there, refused on a rupee account). Client-money coverage's "held for sellers" includes non-rupee seller money at its book value. A remittance from a non-rupee WALLET is refused (`REMITTANCE_SOURCE_CURRENCY_UNSUPPORTED`). **A quote INTO rupees is REFUSED (`TRANSFER_QUOTE_INTO_WALLET_CURRENCY`, 2026-09-12).** A transfer moves a seller's money; it never changes what their wallet owes them. A quote is honoured only INTO another currency, where the credited units carry the rupees that left as their book; INTO rupees a rupee is worth a rupee to the wallet, so they are credited exactly the book value that left and the gap against what arrived is our `FX_SPREAD` — crediting the quoted rupees moved what the book held for them by (quote − average) × units while the wallet stood still. The refusal runs after the idempotent replay, a quote on our own money is unaffected, and the admin transfer modal hides the (system-rate-seeded) field for a seller transfer into an INR account, or every such transfer would hit the refusal.
