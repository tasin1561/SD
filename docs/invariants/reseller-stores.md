**RS-1 (2026-09-14): a reseller store is a `seller_stores` row with `kind = RESELLER`, and `ResellerStoreService` is the ONLY writer of its status.** Transitions are guarded `updateMany` on the status read, with a `reseller_store_events` row in the same tx; approve / reject / close audit HIGH; an admin-created store lands PENDING_SELLER_APPROVAL and the seller is told (in-app + OPERATIONAL email); close is refused while any order on it is in flight. **AMENDED 2026-09-15 (owner): a store closes only from PAUSED, once every parcel is delivered or back in our warehouse, no credit is still to run (WAITING / DUE) and the wallet is ₹0** — `order/services/store-close-rule.ts`, read through `OrderReadService.storeCloseBlockers`. "Done" is NOT "terminal": DELIVERED has a customer-return edge, so the old terminality rule refused every store that had ever delivered a parcel. **Anything asking "is this order finished?" must not read `isTerminal` for DELIVERED.** **CHANNEL stores are unchanged** — every `SellerStoreService` query filters `kind: CHANNEL`, and an order cannot be placed on a reseller store until phase 3 (`STORE_IS_RESELLER`). **AMENDED 2026-09-16 (owner): a store cannot be opened without a CONTACT EMAIL, a CONTACT PHONE and an INVITATION.** All three were optional; a store could exist that nobody could sign in to and nobody could ring — a row that looks open and can do nothing. `contactEmail` / `contactPhone` are required on both create DTOs, `CreateResellerStoreDto.invite` is required (the seller's own store opens ACTIVE, so its first user — the owner — is invited as it is created), and `ApproveResellerStoreDto.invite` is required for the other half: an admin-created store has no team until the seller approves it, and approving is what opens it. `INVITE_AFTER_APPROVAL` still refuses an invite on the admin create. Details: `docs/reseller-stores.md` "Phase 1 as built".

**RS-3 (2026-09-14): a reseller store's catalogue is the seller's terms per (store, variant); what the store SEES of stock is computed on every read and never stored.** Leaf module `reseller-catalogue`. `reseller_price_list_items` is the seller's DEFAULT price per variant; `reseller_store_variants` holds one store's terms (enabled — default false —, a price OVERRIDE that is a whole row or nothing, stock mode SHARED | SET_ASIDE(qty), hidden 0–90 %, overlay title/description/pictures). **RS-4 (2026-09-14): a reseller store's TERMS are append-only versions the store must accept, and `splitFee` is the ONE fee-split arithmetic.** Module `reseller-store-terms` (EXPORTS `ResellerStoreTermsService`, imports nothing order-shaped so phase 3b's order module can import it). `reseller_store_terms_versions` holds, per version, the share of each Skydrop fee the STORE pays (one CHECKed 0–100 column per fee — DELIVERY_FEE, RETURN_FEE, CUSTOMER_RETURN_FEE, COD_FEE, COD_TAX, INSTANT_PAY_FEE; inbound freight is the seller's alone) and each party's credit timing (ON_PAYOUT +N / AFTER_DELIVERY N ≥ 1 / INSTANT / AFTER_CONFIRMATION N); `publish` (`stores.pricing`) numbers versions under `AdvisoryLock.RESELLER_TERMS` with the unique `(store, version)` as backstop, refuses stale (`TERMS_CHANGED`) and unchanged edits, audits MEDIUM before/after, and NEVER updates a row (orders snapshot the version id). `reseller_store_terms_acceptances` (UNIQUE per version, composite FK to the store's own versions) records who, when and from where (`terms.accept`); a new version supersedes acceptance. **Fee-split rule:** store share `round_half_up(fee × pct / 100)` to the paisa, seller pays the REMAINDER, the two always sum to the fee (`terms/fee-split.ts`, pure — never recompute it in a browser; the Terms tab's worked example is served by the API). Fee types are an F2-exhaustive TS union (`reseller-fee-types.ts`). **Credit after confirmation (decision 10)** is the SET-1 key `reseller.credit_after_confirmation_enabled` (seeded false, global row not admin-editable), written ONLY by `PUT /admin/sellers/:sellerId/reseller-credit-after-confirmation` (`reseller.credit_after_confirmation.enable`, reason ≥ 20, HIGH) — `SettingsResolverService` refuses the generic override writers for any key in `DEDICATED_OVERRIDE_KEYS` (`SETTING_HAS_DEDICATED_ENDPOINT`); read through `CreditAfterConfirmationService.isEnabled`, which FAILS CLOSED. Switching it off rewrites no terms: new versions using it are refused, stores whose current version uses it are FLAGGED (derived, never stored) and `orderReadiness` reports `AFTER_CONFIRMATION_NOT_ENABLED`. Phase 3b calls `currentTerms` / `acceptedCurrentTerms` / `orderReadiness` (pass the order's tx) and `splitFeeLines`. Store users are told by email plus a portal-wide banner (no store inbox yet); the seller in-app. Details: `docs/reseller-stores.md` "Terms as built".

The wallet manager cannot change with a claim/withdrawal open (`STORE_WALLET_HAS_OPEN_REQUESTS`), and a store cannot close unless its wallet is ₹0 with nothing open (`STORE_WALLET_NOT_SETTLED`), both under the WALLET lock (close takes it before the row). Negative limit: `store_wallet_settings.negative_limit_inr` (seller-set) capped by `reseller.store_negative_limit_cap_inr` (SET-1, 25,000), cap unreadable ⇒ 0; phase 3b calls `storeCanSpend(tx, …)` INSIDE the transaction that writes the debit. Store orders and their money are NOT in this wave.

a store's repeated CSV reference is an error row, never a PATCH (ORD-9 for stores). **Confirmation respects set-asides for EVERY order**: M5 `reserve({ guard })` runs `ResellerStockGateService.guardFor` inside the reservation's own transaction under `AdvisoryLock.RESELLER_SET_ASIDE` — SET_ASIDE store `min(own unused, real)`, SHARED store `real − Σ other stores' unused`, **CHANNEL (seller's own) order `real − Σ all stores' unused`** (a behaviour change for sellers with set-asides; No provisional state and no re-lock (a store ledger waits on no nightly job). Invariant pinned by `store-pnl-carry-forward.spec.ts`: Σ frozen months + Σ carry-forwards + open month live = live span, per line. **`store_expenses`** is the store's own book (`StoreExpenseService`; IDEM-1 key; soft delete with a reason, never edited; dated not before the store's first month nor in the future; never a bank or wallet entry; the seller never reads it). Seller `stores.reports` (scorecards, ranking, transfer revenue by store — the seller's wallet entries naming the store's orders, signed by `isSellerWalletCredit`, the ONE credit set — and the stock forecast); `PnlService` is untouched. **RS-9 (2026-09-15): analysis, auto-pause and fraud signals.** Scorecards are pure (`reseller-scorecard.ts`): rates divide only by orders whose outcome is known (confirmation ÷ decided, delivery/return ÷ delivered+returned+lost), margin = (transfer − the seller's unit cost) × qty where the cost is known, coverage stated. **Auto-pause** is the seller's rule (`reseller_store_auto_pause`, `stores.manage`): hourly, an ACTIVE store whose returned ÷ (delivered + returned) over the window is ABOVE the limit with at least the minimum outcomes is paused through `ResellerStoreService.autoPause` — the ONE status writer, a SYSTEM actor, the same guarded PAUSE and audit — and the seller is told in-app (`seller.reseller_store_auto_paused`). After the seller resumes, only outcomes since the resume count. **Fraud flags** (`reseller-fraud-rules.ts`, pure) — cancel, return and failed-delivery rates, rapid-fire orders, retail above suggested, one customer phone across stores — against GLOBAL `reseller.fraud_*` settings; the admin list and the daily sweep read the SAME computation; the sweep raises MEDIUM (HIGH at twice the threshold) `RESELLER_RISK` issues keyed `reseller-fraud:<rule>:<store>` and resolves them when back under. Staff act with the new dangerous `reseller.stores.pause` (resume stays the seller's). **Stock forecast**: days of stock = sellable available ÷ (units confirmed in `reseller.stock_forecast_window_days` ÷ days), reorder below `reseller.stock_reorder_days` (both SET-1), a weekly in-app digest (`seller.reseller_stock_reorder`). Admin float per seller and store reads WAL-9's `InstantPayAdvanceService` (now exported by TreasuryModule) rather than restating its predicate.

A COD credit is FRONTED from capital (reference = the order id, WAL-9); a courier payout on a reseller order is wholly capital's; held = max(0, seller + Σ stores) after every step (11 reseller scenarios in `settlement-bank-invariant.spec.ts`). **Channel orders are byte-identical**: every hook (`OrderChargesAccrualService`, `OrderChargesRefundService`, `RtoFeeAccrualService`, `AccrualExecutionService`, `CourierSettlementService`) branches on `isResellerOrder` / `head` / `storeKind` and a channel order takes today's code. **Store actions (2026-09-16, reworked 2026-09-17, WIDENED to the whole order 2026-09-18, owner): a Reseller store acts on its OWN live orders, and the SELLER decides per store, per task, whether the store may do it at all and — if so — whether it happens directly or needs Seller staff's approval. "The approve and reject is done by seller not by skydrop."** `reseller_store_action_policy` is one row per store, set by the seller (`GET|PUT /seller/reseller-stores/:storeId/action-policy`, `stores.manage`, MEDIUM audit carrying the EFFECTIVE policy either side) — one column per capability (`recall`, `orderChange`, `cancel`, `callCapDecision`, `chaseSkydrop`, `reattempt`, `sendBack`), each OFF | ASK_SELLER | DIRECT. **`orderChange` was `addressFix` until 2026-09-18** — the SAME column (`address_fix`, kept so no store loses the setting its seller chose; `CAPABILITY_COLUMN` maps the two and the spec reads the migration through it), the same three modes and the same default, WIDENED from "correct the address" to "change the order". One switch, because "may this store change its order, and do I see it first?" is one question. **Seller staff are asked it as TWO questions per task** — "Can the Reseller store do this?" Yes/No, and only on Yes "How?" Directly / Needs my approval (No = OFF, Yes + approval = ASK_SELLER, Yes + directly = DIRECT); only the screen asks it that way. **All seven support all three answers; nothing on that screen is a choice the server refuses.** **A MISSING ROW IS THE DEFAULTS, never "nothing allowed"**: `DEFAULT_POLICY` and the migration's column defaults must agree (`reseller-store-action-policy.spec.ts` reads the migration), and `cancel` defaults DIRECT because stores already held `orders.cancel`. **A capability is a COLUMN, never a row keyed by an enum value.** **What DIRECT does differs per task, and the screen says it**: a recall queues our call centre and opens a ticket; a re-attempt opens a ticket whose courier message Skydrop admin sends by hand; **a send-back calls the courier's cancel on the store's click and turns the parcel round with nobody checking** (CUR-10's seller amendment — the copy used to say "Skydrop still carries it out", which was never true); cancel, an order change, a call-cap answer and an issue apply at once. **ASK_SELLER holds a request, in one of THREE tables**: delivery asks in `order_delivery_action_requests` (they carry a shipment), a store's ORDER CHANGE in `store_address_change_requests` (a column per recipient field PLUS a `patch` JSONB carrying the WHOLE proposed change since 2026-09-18 — items, quantities, retail, the money — because a change that moves products cannot be expressed as columns without one per DTO field; the recipient columns stay written so every existing reader keeps working, and the patch is the authority when present. The proposal never sits on the order, and approving re-runs the SAME validation a direct change does, so a variant archived meanwhile is refused by name rather than written), and **cancel / call-cap answer / issue with Skydrop in ONE shared table `store_order_requests`** (`kind` CANCEL | CALL_CAP_DECISION | RAISE_ISSUE, status PENDING → APPROVED | REJECTED | EXPIRED, APPROVED → EXECUTED | FAILED; `note`, `cancellation_reason`, `call_cap_proposal`, `issue_subject`; one open per order per kind under `AdvisoryLock.STORE_ORDER_REQUEST` → `STORE_REQUEST_ALREADY_OPEN`) — one table because the three share the whole lifecycle and three near-identical tables would drift. The owner reversed two old refusals: a seller may approve a store's issue before it reaches Skydrop, and a store may PROPOSE a call-cap answer. The store's endpoints reply with a UNION `{applied:true, …, request:null} | {applied:false, …:null, request}` so the portal says what happened from the reply. `store-order-request` is an R3 PRIMITIVE (record, notifier, sweep; imports nothing order/ticket/review-shaped, imported by `reseller-order`, `early-reservation-decision`, `ticket`, `order-core`); the LEAF `store-order-request-decision` approves by running the SAME call DIRECT runs, as the STORE (`cancelBySeller` with `ActorType.STORE`, `decideAsStore`, `openStoreIssue`). **Seller staff decide, never Skydrop admin**: the admin delivery-action claim predicate carries `needsSellerApproval: false` (a held row is `DELIVERY_ACTION_HELD_FOR_SELLER`); admin see those rows read-only (`waitingOnSeller`). Every seller claim is a guarded `updateMany` on (PENDING, this seller's) → `…_ALREADY_DECIDED`; answers land in `decided_by_seller_user_id` / `seller_decided_at`, never a staff column; a rejection needs a reason. **Approving: RE-CHECK, RUN, RECORD THE TRUE OUTCOME, THEN EMAIL — in that order.** A request that no longer applies (delivery ask: order no longer OUT_FOR_DELIVERY/DELIVERY_FAILED or its live shipment changed, `stillApplies`; cancel: `cancelBySeller`'s refusals; call cap: review no longer OPEN; address: `edit`'s refusals) runs NOTHING and is FAILED with the refusal verbatim; a throw is FAILED too — no APPROVED row is ever left that did nothing; the store's email carries the real `outcome`. `DeliveryActionService.runApproved` is the ONE path for an approved delivery ask (an approved recall opens the ticket a direct one does; admin's recall uses it too); a courier refusal writes `execution_error` and keeps seller staff's note. **Unanswered requests expire**: `StoreRequestExpiryService` (queue `store-request-expiry`, every 15 min, SCALE-1) over ALL THREE queues reminds Seller staff once in-app after `reseller.store_request_remind_hours` (24; claim `seller_reminded_at IS NULL` first) and after `reseller.store_request_expire_hours` (72) closes it EXPIRED by a guarded `updateMany` on PENDING (an approval landing first wins) and emails the store (`store.request_expired.email`) — aged from `created_at`, never `updated_at`. **BOTH SIDES MAY CHANGE THE WHOLE ORDER (owner, 2026-09-18).** Seller staff reach every field (`OrderService.edit`, no key restriction); the store reaches every field but two, and each is closed for a reason about the FIELD rather than about who is asking (`STORE_FORBIDDEN_KEYS`: `internalNotes`, Skydrop's own working notes; `storeId`, because moving an order to another shopfront moves its money, its customer identity and its terms to a deal never struck for it) — a DENY list on purpose, so a field added to the DTO later is not silently closed to the store. `EDIT_DURING_CALL` is GONE: the change is written to the timeline saying an agent was on the phone, and the call station re-reads what it holds and says on screen that it moved (it was reading a snapshot the refusal never protected). Past `CONTENTS_EDITABLE_STATUSES` the CONTENTS are refused for both (`NOT_EDITABLE` — stock held, waybill booked, box maybe packed) while the RECIPIENT is routed by `recipientChangeRoute` — the ONE place, and it asks a FACT (does a live shipment carry a waybill?) rather than reading a status list: no waybill ⇒ ours to write, a waybill ⇒ `COURIER_MUST_ACCEPT_ADDRESS_CHANGE` naming the endpoint that asks them. **A courier-accepted change is written to the shipment AND the order; a refusal writes neither and both sides are told the courier's own words** — never store an address the parcel is not going to. The store's post-handover ask is `POST /store/orders/:id/consignee`, and it is the ONE place the store is narrower than Seller staff: ASK_SELLER is refused there by name (`STORE_ACTION_NEEDS_SELLER_NOW`), because a held request applied a day later may miss the courier's own window entirely and would be an approval that silently does nothing. **The money follows**: `ResellerOrderMoneyService.recalculateAfterEdit` re-plans under the order's SNAPSHOTTED terms and the COD rates STAMPED on the plan (`gst_percent_at_plan` &c — re-resolving them would let last week's settings move an older order); a credit row that has written nothing is rewritten in place, guarded on the status it was read in. **A CREDITED row is REFUSED BY NAME** (`RESELLER_CREDIT_ALREADY_PAID`, the whole transaction rolled back so a party re-planned earlier in the same pass is rolled back with it, and a HIGH `reseller_order.money_recalculation_refused` audit row written OUTSIDE the transaction, or it would roll back too). It cannot arise — the CONTENTS freeze at confirmation and a reseller credit runs at or after delivery, so the only edit `edit` then accepts is the RECIPIENT, which moves no figure; the only way in is god mode putting a paid order back where its lines are changeable, which is a bypass. **The REFUSAL stands on its own reasoning, and that is now the only thing holding it.** It used to be backed by the database as well — the index covered `cod_collection`, so re-writing a credit was unrepresentable. Since 2026-10-13 it is not (WAL-10): the index no longer covers the COD-credit family, because WAL-6 says a reversed-and-re-paid COD IS credited again and the contradiction was aborting whole courier payouts. So a re-credit is now writable, and `RESELLER_CREDIT_ALREADY_PAID` refuses it because a half-re-priced plan is worse than an unchanged one — not because the database would. **Do not restore the index to re-create that backstop**: it would reintroduce the payout-aborting P2002 for every reseller order, which is the bug WAL-10 exists to fix. Changing how a PRICED order is paid for is refused by name (`RESELLER_PAYMENT_MODE_LOCKED`) rather than guessed. A store correction waiting on that order closes SUPERSEDED in the same transaction. **Whoever did not make the change is told, every time, with old → new and the money's before and after**: the store IN-APP (`store.order_changed_by_seller`, `store.customer_changed_by_seller` — by email too until 2026-09-20, when the store inbox made the second channel a duplicate), Seller staff IN-APP by PERMISSION under `seller.store_changed_order`. **A store's action is attributed to the STORE the whole way down** (`courierActor.store`, the ticket's `storeId`/`openedByStoreUserId`, `CallQueueReason.STORE_ASKED`, `store.*` audit actions), including after seller approval. **The store hears IN-APP** — `store.action_approved|rejected`, `store.address_change_approved|rejected`, `store.request_approved|rejected|expired`, `store.order_changed_by_seller`, `store.customer_changed_by_seller`. **This paragraph said "by EMAIL only (no inbox)" until 2026-09-20**, which was true when it was written and stopped being true the day the store inbox landed (2026-09-19); the nine email legs are now in `RETIRED_EMAIL_TEMPLATES` and each names the topic that carries it. **Its CREDENTIAL mail stays** — a store user with no session cannot read an inbox (NOTIF-9).  Seller staff hear in-app by PERMISSION `stores.manage` under `seller.store_action_waiting`, `seller.store_address_change_waiting`, `seller.store_request_waiting`, `seller.store_request_reminder` and `seller.store_changed_order` (all catalogued, NOTIF-17); no notifier throws. **`seller.store_request_reminder` CANNOT BE SILENCED** (owner, 2026-09-18): it is named in `IMMUTABLE_TOPICS` on `NotificationPolicyService` — a per-topic exception rather than a new category, because a category decides channels too, would have to be routed in `categoryForTemplate`, and would then be available to attach to anything. Enforced in BOTH halves — `assertMutable` refuses to record the mute and `mutesFor` ignores one already stored — since a rule held only at the write is a rule with a back door for every row already in the table. The other three stay mutable. The nav badge counts all three queues (`store-requests-count.spec.ts` pins each list predicate to the count). Details: `docs/reseller-stores.md` "Store actions as built".

**ASSOC-1 (2026-10-08, owner): an ASSOCIATE sells for a reseller store, and is a STORE USER with a narrow role and a SCOPE — never a fourth identity and never a tier.** Design: `docs/associates.md`; app `apps/associate` at `associate.skydrop.global` (`portal.skydrop.global` until the 2026-10-13 rename, and still serving — see CON-1; port **3007**, pm2 `skydrop-associate` — **not** `skydrop-portal`, which is the courier-portal Chromium worker and must stay separate; **3006 was the obvious next number and is already `apps/marketing`'s**, and two apps on one port collide in `pnpm dev` and, far worse, in Playwright's `webServer` array, where the second to boot finds the first already answering and every spec then runs against the WRONG SITE and PASSES — check the root `playwright.config.ts` for the port before picking one). Owner: *"the associate will generate the reseller sales, then the reseller will know which associate is performing how much and then he can decide what to do."*

**Nothing is a tier, and that is what keeps three invariants intact.** An associate has NO wallet (the reseller settles with them outside Skydrop), so RS-4's `splitFee` still has exactly one remainder-holder, TRE-8c's bank invariant still walks ONE level of store wallets (`held for a seller = max(0, seller wallet + Σ that seller's store wallets)`), and RS-3's visible stock is still a set-aside rather than a set-aside inside one. **A wallet for an associate is a different size of project and that invariant is the first thing it has to answer.**

**THE TWO PRIVACY BOUNDARIES, and each is STRUCTURAL rather than a filter somebody must remember.** (a) The SELLER's unit cost is withheld from the store, which was already true in two places and had **no test naming either** — `StoreCatalogueItem` does not carry the field and `StoreAnalysisService.analysis()` passes `unitCostInr: null`; a `: null` with nothing pinning it is one refactor from becoming a value, silently, because nothing fails when a privacy boundary opens. Now pinned. (b) The STORE's cost and earnings are withheld from the associate **by PERMISSION**: `catalogue.view` carries `transferPriceInr`, the associate does not hold it, and `catalogue.sell` is the same catalogue with the store's cost removed and the associate's own retail in its place — a SECOND endpoint with a narrower projection, built from its own explicit type, because a filter inside the wide one is something somebody has to remember on every field added afterwards. `terms.view` is withheld for the same reason (the fee split and credit timing make the store's margin derivable), as are `reports.view` and `wallet.*`. `associate-response-boundary.spec.ts` is the backstop: every associate-reachable route either sweeps clean of cost and earnings fields or names itself with the reason it is safe — **neither is the default**, so the decision is forced in the commit that adds the field (the IMP-4 lesson, where a deny list that made things safe by default protected only the routes it was not needed for). **The sweep runs TWICE over different evidence, because each half misses what the other catches**: over the real response's keys AND over the DECLARED interface source with comments stripped first (the RBAC-1 lesson, where a spec asserted on a docblock and passed while testing nothing) — a field declared and populated only on another path is invisible to the first, and a field only on the wire is invisible to the second. It also compares VALUES, not only names: a field called `basePriceInr` carrying 47.25 passes every name check and leaks the identical fact. **It found THREE leaks on its first run, all closed in the same change, and none of them was written as a bug**: the order view's per-line `transferPriceInr` / retail range, `GET /store/orders/:id/money`, and RS-7's `disputedFigures` on the ticket routes — the same fact by three routes, each correct for a store whose whole team held `terms.view`. The first and third became **scope-discriminated unions** (`StoreOrderLineView.cost`, `StoreTicketView.figures`: `{visible:true, …} | {visible:false}`), so a reader must narrow before touching a figure and **a cost field added to that arm later is unreachable at OWN scope BY THE TYPE SYSTEM** rather than by somebody remembering a filter; `figures` was RENAMED from `disputedFigures` so a consumer still reading the old key gets a type error, not `undefined`. The second is refused outright — 403 `STORE_MONEY_NOT_FOR_ASSOCIATE`, **403 and not 404 because the order exists and is theirs to follow; it is the FIGURE that is refused** — rather than narrowed field by field, which would leave a screen of blanks. **The sweep reads DECLARED types, which is its strength and its one blind spot: it cannot see WHICH ARM ships.** So a union's leaf fields stay flagged for ever, and emptying the open list to silence that would be a lie. `WITHHELD_BY_SCOPE` is the THIRD state — declared, never sent — and it is only honest because **every entry NAMES the e2e that proves absence against a real database, and a test fails if that file does not exist or does not cover the route it claims**; without that the list is the hole, since anybody could move an inconvenient finding into it and the gate would go green on a sentence. Both negative cases were proven to fire. `DELIBERATELY_VISIBLE` holds exactly one entry (`walletManagedBy` — WHO manages the wallet, never a figure) and both lists are keyed per **(route, field)**, not per route: clearing a route would let a field added next month inherit an exemption written for a different one.

**Scope is a COLUMN on the ROLE (`store_roles.order_scope`, OWN | ALL), resolved in ONE place, applied in the WHERE clause.** `storeOrderScope(roles)` is that place and **WIDEST WINS** — adding a role must never take something away (RBAC-1b's ANY-semantics in the other direction), so ALL beside OWN is ALL and an OWNER role is ALL whatever its column says. Resolved in the guard over the SAME live roles the permissions came from — `resolveRoles` now returns `live`, the caller's own selected rows, so the `deletedAt` filter stays in one place and a guard whose permissions and whose scope disagree about which roles count is unrepresentable. **Never compare a role key at a call site**: a person holds several roles, and `roleKeys.includes('associate')` would be narrowed by a role that was only ever meant to grant more. It narrows orders AND customers — "only his customers" is "a customer this person has placed an order for", because identity is per OWNER (ORD-7) and two associates selling to one phone share a row, so the scope is over the ORDERS. **ALL is stated explicitly on the five seeded roles by the migration** rather than left to the column default: the default is `own` (fail-closed for a role added later), and defaulting the existing five would have silently narrowed every store login to "only orders I placed myself" — which, since no order placed before today records a store user at all, reads as every store suddenly having no orders. `provisionDefaultStoreRoles` writes it for every role for the same reason: a default is what a role gets when nobody decided.

**`orders.placed_by_store_user_id` is NULLABLE for ever, and null means "no store user placed this"** — a seller's own order, staff, a store API key (which carries no person) — **never "unknown"**. Every order placed before today genuinely has no answer and a backfilled guess would be a claim about who sold something.

**`store_users.orders_paused_at` gates `orders.create` ONLY** (RS-1's PAUSED semantics one level down): no NEW orders, everything already placed carries on, and they keep reading, tracking, cancelling and chasing it. Re-read per request with the roles, so switching it off takes effect on their next call rather than when their token expires. Refused `ASSOCIATE_ORDERS_PAUSED` at the create boundary, before anything is written.

**`associate_prices` is per (associate, variant), SET BY HAND, with no markup rule and no fallback** (owner: A and B may sell the same product at different prices, and the price is FIXED — the associate cannot change it at order time). A product with no row is refused BY NAME (`ASSOCIATE_PRICE_NOT_SET`) rather than priced at something nobody chose. **The seller's terms bound it**: a price outside the store variant's retail range is refused `ASSOCIATE_PRICE_OUT_OF_SELLER_RANGE` **at the moment the reseller sets it** — *no associate can break the seller's terms*, and the person who can fix it is looking at the screen now rather than hearing about it weeks later at an order. A range that MOVES under an existing price leaves it out of range, reported and never auto-adjusted: a price somebody negotiated is not ours to change. **The range is read from `ResellerStockGateService.offersFor`, never re-derived** — "override ?? default" is decided per ROW (RS-3), and a second implementation is how the pricing screen comes to show a range the order gate does not enforce. On a copy-from, an out-of-range line is a NAMED SKIP rather than a refusal of the whole copy — refusing fifty prices over one is how a store gives up and sets none. The refusals apply to a caller whose resolved scope is OWN; an ALL-scope store user places exactly as they do today, so every existing store order is byte-identical.

**The invitation is the EXISTING store invitation with the role `associate`** — nothing new in that table, and there is no `/store/associates/invitations` (an early draft of the design doc named one; it was never built). **Which app the link points at is a DECISION** (`invitationAppUrl`): the two portals are separate ORIGINS and `__Host-storeRefresh` is bound to the one that set it, so an associate who accepted at the reseller origin would hold a session the portal cannot read. Widest wins again — associate AND NOTHING ELSE goes to `ASSOCIATE_APP_URL`, any wider set to the reseller app, and a RESEND reads the keys back so it cannot point somewhere the original did not. **The three associate MUTATIONS are refused during a support session** (`/store/associates` on `FORBIDDEN_WHILE_IMPERSONATING`, IMP-4): a price sets what every later order of that person sells at and the pause switch decides whether a third party may trade at all — the store's own commercial decisions about their staff, each outlasting the session, each one click away on their own screen. The READS are untouched, and the sweep covers mutating routes only, so the prefix never blinds a GET. **That sweep forced the decision on its first run after the routes landed, which is what it is for.** The role is a sixth FIXED key, not one a store invents: the store side is key-based on purpose (`@IsIn(STORE_ROLE_KEYS)` is the complete vocabulary because `provisionDefaultStoreRoles` is the only writer), and RBAC-1b records that the day store-role CREATION lands, that binding becomes exactly the staff bug where nobody could be invited onto a role their own team had made.

**Capability 4 of the owner's list — a per-pincode rate check — is NOT built, and that is a decision on record rather than an omission.** Pricing has been a FLAT per-seller fee since the zone/slab engine priced a production order at ₹0.00 (PRC-1..8), so there are no per-pincode rates to check; building one is a pricing-engine project with its own invariants, its own seed data and its own failure mode.
